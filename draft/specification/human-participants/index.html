    Human Participants and the Trusted Surface¶

    Version 2026-10-18

    This page is part of BACP version 2026-10-18. Rules marked Proposed depend on an open question and may change in a later dated release.

    Every BACP guarantee that a human agreed to something ends at the Trusted Surface: mandates are approved there, step-ups are confirmed there, and every fail-safe default escalates there. This page says who the human participants are, what the Trusted Surface MUST do, how what it showed is bound to what the principal approved, and how a principal reaches a human. It also records which of these rules are BACP policy and which rest on a law in force.

    Roles¶

    An agent acts for a principal. Other humans, and other agents, may take part in the same session. Each is listed in Session.participants[] as a Participant (participant_id, role, ref) from common.json ($defs/Participant).

    Role (ParticipantRole) Who How the role shows in the evidence
    principal The person or business the agent acts for. Referred to by a pairwise pseudonymous reference, never by an Aadhaar number. Approves on the Trusted Surface; log actor: user
    assisted_operator A human who completes the session for the principal at a physical touchpoint, such as a village-level entrepreneur, a business correspondent or a kirana operator (channel: assisted). Presentation.assisted_by; log actor: assisted_operator with actor_ref
    seller_operator A human acting for the seller: a kirana owner, a munim, a sales officer. Log actor: seller_operator; typed actor on ledger acknowledgements and trade acts
    sub_agent An agent acting under a mandate delegated by the session's agent (parent_mandate_id, which only narrows). Log actor: sub_agent with actor_ref

    Two kinds of human act outside a session:

    • Seller staff and support staff act through the seller app (BPP) and IGM. They are not session participants and not InstructionLog actors. Whether IGM gets an actor type for an AI agent, or for seller staff, is open (Q-11).
    • A human support operator of the agent operator accepts and releases a handoff. The operator's key is a registry key of role support_operator, and it signs only under the non-agent tag bacp-handoff (Signature tags). Its registry record names the agent operator and one person, with one key per person and no shared desk keys (Registry lookup; Q-64, settled).

    Accessibility needs travel only as Presentation.modality. A participant record MUST NOT carry disability data.

    The Trusted Surface is a role¶

    The Trusted Surface is a role, not an app. A payer PSP's app, a Mandate Manager's app, a PSP's IVR callback or an assisted counter run by a PSP or Mandate Manager is a Trusted Surface if it meets TS-1 to TS-6 below. In every case:

    • it is non-agentic: no agent drives it, reads the factor the principal enters, or posts its events;
    • its events reach the gateway only at POST /trusted-surface/events, signed under tag bacp-ts with the registry key of the step-up's Mandate Manager or payer PSP (Step-up signers; Signature tags);
    • it takes the content of a step-up only from the gateway, by step_up_id, never from the agent (Render input).

    Step-up signers¶

    Only two registry keys may fetch a step-up's render and post its result: those of the step-up's Mandate Manager and of its payer PSP (for a summary step-up, the Mandate Manager's only). The gateway determines them from signed mandates, never from the request:

    Step-up Mandate Manager Payer PSP
    A session step-up (confirm, rail_auth) The issuer of the session's mandate (the open Checkout Mandate) The PSP named in rail_binding.psp_id of any of the leg's planned open Payment Mandates: those under the session's root that the gateway planned to pay this session with, more than one under split tender (Per-leg rule). Each names the Checkout Mandate in its checkout_mandate_ref
    A summary step-up over a multi-seller basket (covers[] present) The issuer of the shared root open Checkout Mandate None: no payer PSP fetches or answers a summary, so that no PSP learns the rest of the basket
    A post_order step-up The issuer of the mandate the order was placed under The PSP named in auth_binding.psp_id of the order's closed Payment Mandate; under split tender, of any of the order's closed Payment Mandates
    A negotiation confirm step-up The issuer of the negotiation mandate named in the negotiation's mandate_ref.id None: the approval releases no payment
    A post_order step-up for an order-less ledger act (LEDGER_ACK of a ledger_ref or a ledger_range) The issuer of the mandate named in actor.mandate_ref of the request, which a request for such an act MUST carry; the gateway checks that the mandate's principal is the counterparty of the poster of every entry before it raises the step-up (On the wire: in.bacp.ledger) None: there is no closed Payment Mandate

    A Checkout Mandate carries no PSP binding, and a closed Payment Mandate exists only from complete, so the payer PSP of a session step-up is never taken from an auth_binding. On a human-present session there is no open Payment Mandate, so a session step-up has no payer PSP: only its Mandate Manager fetches the render and posts the result. The payment.afa_completed event, which is not a step-up result, is signed by the PSP named in auth_binding.psp_id of the session's closed Payment Mandate (under split tender, of any of the session's closed Payment Mandates) under tag bacp-ts. Any other registry key gets 404 not_found, never 403, and nothing changes.

    Origins and delivery¶

    The registry record of every Mandate Manager and payer PSP (registry-record.json) carries the places where its Trusted Surface can be reached. The fields are optional unless stated, and a registry that cannot carry them is open (Registry lookup):

    • ts_origins[]: https origins only (scheme, host and optional port, no path; RFC 6454). A tel: number is never an origin.
    • ts_notify_url: an https endpoint for a content-free push.
    • ts_call_numbers[]: E.164 numbers from which the Trusted Surface calls. They are informative.
    • ts_languages[] and ts_modalities[]: the languages and modalities that the Trusted Surface claims, so that the language fail-safe can be tested.

    The gateway delivers a step-up by these rules:

    1. Origin. The gateway mints trusted_surface_url only on an origin in ts_origins[] of the step-up's signer (Step-up signers), and sets StepUp.surface to that signer's subscriber id so that a client can show the host to the principal and check it against the subscriber. It accepts a Mandate Manager's mandate-approval link only if it is an https URL on an origin of that Mandate Manager, or a rail deep link whose scheme the rail defines, which opens the PSP's own app.
    2. Content-free push. If no origin of the signer conforms and the signer has a ts_notify_url, the gateway sets delivery: app_push and sends a gateway-signed POST of {step_up_id, expires_at} to that URL, signed under RFC 9421 with a key in its profile's keys, as it signs a webhook (Request signatures). The push carries no content, no link and no text. The Trusted Surface fetches the render by step_up_id as for any step-up (Render input). The step-up carries no trusted_surface_url and session.updated carries no link. The agent is not a party to the push.
    3. Otherwise the gateway answers 503 trusted_surface_unavailable, reason: no_conforming_origin, with resolution requires_buyer_input. The code is never recoverable, because a model reads a bare 503 as "retry" (Errors). With reason: language_unavailable or reason: channel_not_allowed the error carries the session's handoff_routes[].

    ts_call_numbers[] is a hint that an agent may speak ("this bank calls from these numbers"). It is never an authenticator, because caller identity can be spoofed. An agent that speaks it labels it "calls from" and always adds that the bank never asks for the PIN on a call it makes. Whether a PSP may publish the numbers it calls from is open (Q-63).

    An agent SHOULD check, before it shows the principal a trusted_surface_url, that the host of the URL is an origin of the subscriber in StepUp.surface, and an MCP client that does URL-mode elicitation shows that host as the MCP specification requires (MCP and A2A).

    Requirements TS-1 to TS-6¶

    A Trusted Surface MUST meet all six:

    • TS-1. Signed and templated content only. It renders a step-up only from the gateway-signed ts_render and its own templates. Text from a seller or other counterparty appears only in the labelled counterparty_text slot of ts_render, rendered as visibly labelled seller text (in quote marks or after a label such as "Seller's text:"), never interpolated into a template sentence and never as the Trusted Surface's own words.
    • TS-2. Decline is as easy as approve. Declining takes no more steps than approving; no option is preselected; and expiry means decline. The decline control carries a neutral label (for example "Decline" or "No"), with no fear, guilt or shame wording, and the Trusted Surface never asks the principal to confirm a decline ("Are you sure?"). A PIN is entered only as the PIN rule allows.
    • TS-3. The principal's language and modality. It renders in the principal's language (English or a language of the Eighth Schedule) and modality (visual, audio or both). Audio is read in full before the principal enters the factor. On every modality, before the factor, from its own templates and never from seller text, it presents a step-up that pays for an order in this order: (1) the total; (2) each fee line one at a time, with its recipient where charge_lines[] states one; (3) everything else (goods, delivery, packing and tax) as one figure. Fee lines are the codes convenience, platform, payment_handling, handling and cod_fee, every line of type other_charge, and every operator charge, whoever receives them. The remainder is goods, delivery, packing and tax only. The gateway builds charge_lines[] from the lines of the quote bound by checkout_hash and from operator_charges[], and the remainder never contains a fee line. An order with no fee line adds no audio. It does not read a per-handler comparison on an IVR or audio-only surface. It never reads intermediary_compensation[] per order.
    • TS-4. No urgency. It shows no urgency or countdown text. It computes any timing it shows from expires_at itself.
    • TS-5. Record a Presentation. It records a Presentation for every approval and decline, and sends it inside its signed event.
    • TS-6. Render a step-up only to the principal or payer it names. A Trusted Surface renders a step-up only to the principal (Mandate Manager) or payer (payer PSP) that the render names. Otherwise it shows nothing of the content, and it posts nothing. A Mandate Manager's surface checks that ts_render.mandate_id names a mandate of the principal who is logged in. A payer PSP's surface checks that ts_render.payer_ref equals the sha-256 over the JCS form of {step_up_id, payer_vpa} that it computes for the payer who is logged in. Each signer binds what it can check: the Mandate Manager knows the mandate, and the payer PSP knows only the payer. On a mismatch the Trusted Surface shows only the fixed sentence below, in ts_render.language (or, if it cannot render that language, in the principal's language), enters no factor and records a conformance failure for rule TS-6. On IVR the sentence is read and the call ends without a factor being taken.
    Language Sentence
    en-IN "This request is for a different account. Do not enter anything here."
    hi-IN "यह अनुरोध किसी दूसरे खाते के लिए है। यहाँ कुछ भी न डालें।"

    The hi-IN sentence says, in English: "This request is for a different account. Do not enter anything here." A Trusted Surface in another language uses a fixed translation of the same sentence from its own templates. TS-6 closes the case in which a forwarded link opens on a surface that is logged in as someone else: TS-1 to TS-5 bind a surface to a signed render, never to the person. A principal who enters her own PIN on another person's request makes a two-factor-compliant debit, which is why the rule is a MUST.

    Language fail-safe¶

    A Trusted Surface that cannot render ts_render.language MUST NOT render another language unless the principal chooses it on the Trusted Surface (the approval then carries Presentation.language_choice: principal_selected). Otherwise it posts nothing, and the gateway answers 503 trusted_surface_unavailable with reason: language_unavailable and the session's HandoffRoute[]. The gateway sets ts_render.language from the mandate, never from an agent's request. The DPDP Act, 2023, s.6(3) (the option of English or an Eighth Schedule language, from 13-05-2027) is cited as alignment, not as the source of the rule. A principal whose language no Trusted Surface serves gets a human route, not self-service.

    PIN rule¶

    A UPI PIN is taken only on a PSP-operated surface: the PSP's app, or a call that the principal places to the PSP's published IVR number. It is never taken on a web page, and never on a call that the Trusted Surface places. This is BACP policy. That NPCI rules already say so is not verified as of 18-10-2026. A Trusted Surface that places a call can still confirm a non-money step-up by voice, and tells the principal in its own words to call the PSP's own number to enter a PIN.

    Render input: ts_render¶

    For every step-up the gateway creates a ts_render: a compact JWS, signed with the gateway's BAP registry key, over the JCS form of the payload below. The gateway signs it once, when it creates the step-up, and serves it byte-identical on every fetch: a ts_render never changes for a step_up_id. A gateway that re-signed a different render for the same step_up_id would fail its own hash check on the approval (403 ts_render_mismatch).

    The session's step_up object carries it, and the Trusted Surface fetches it with GET /trusted-surface/step-ups/{step_up_id}, signed under tag bacp-ts:

    • The gateway MUST serve the render only to the step-up's Mandate Manager or payer PSP (Step-up signers).
    • Any other Mandate Manager or PSP registry key gets 404 not_found, never 403, so that the endpoint does not reveal which step-ups exist. An agent gets 403 trusted_surface_only.
    • The gateway rate-limits the fetch and answers an excess with 429. It counts the limit per caller key, before it looks up the step_up_id, so that a 429 never depends on whether the step-up exists.

    The trusted_surface_url that the agent delivers carries only a reference to the step-up, never the content to show. The payload is defined by ts-render.json, and the OpenAPI component TsRender refers to it.

    • Typed JWS. The JWS protected header of a ts_render MUST carry typ: bacp-ts-render+jws (RFC 8725 §3.11), and a Trusted Surface MUST NOT render a JWS with any other typ or none. The name follows the bacp-<name>+<suffix> convention of bacp-conformance+jwt. There is no v field: typ and the versioned schema identify the version.
    • Per-kind rules. The schema makes these rules testable: a post_order render carries action and order_id (an order-less LEDGER_ACK has none) and no session_id; a session render carries session_id with its checkout_hash, and a negotiation render carries offer_hash, never both hashes; a render with covers[] carries basket[] and no seller_name; a RETURN carries items[]; and an issue action carries issue_id. step_up_id is at least 128 bits from a cryptographically secure random source (^stp_[A-Za-z0-9_-]{22,64}$), opaque and never read aloud to or typed by a person.
    • Must-understand is scoped. A render MAY carry render_required[], naming the fields that the principal must be shown, for example catalogue_disclosure, items, unrequested_lines or acceptance_revocation. A Trusted Surface that does not understand a name listed there renders nothing and posts nothing. It ignores an unknown field that is not listed, so that adding a render field does not break every deployed Trusted Surface. The gateway refuses an approval whose presentation.renders omits a listed name with 400 invalid_request (Presentation record). Presentation.renders is the only extension point of Presentation.
    Payload field Content
    step_up_id, session_id The step-up and its session. session_id is absent for kind: post_order, which has no session.
    kind, reason As in the step_up object (Step-up).
    mandate_id REQUIRED. The mandate the step-up concerns: the session's open Checkout Mandate, or for post_order the mandate the order was placed under (for an order-less LEDGER_ACK, the mandate named in actor.mandate_ref). A Mandate Manager's surface renders the step-up only if the mandate belongs to the principal who is logged in (TS-6).
    payer_ref REQUIRED exactly when Step-up signers gives the step-up a payer PSP (an HNP session step-up with a planned open Payment Mandate, or a post_order step-up of an order with a closed Payment Mandate), and absent otherwise. The sha-256 over the JCS form of {step_up_id, payer_vpa}, where payer_vpa is taken from the Payment Mandate that makes the PSP a signer. A payer PSP's surface renders the step-up only if the hash equals the one it computes for the payer who is logged in (TS-6).
    action, order_id, issue_id For kind: post_order only: the write the principal confirms, the order (absent for an order-less LEDGER_ACK), and for an issue action the issue (Presence proof). action is exactly one of: CANCEL, RETURN, RAISE_ISSUE; for an issue action, the issue action's own action value from POST /issues/{issue_id}/actions (INFO_PROVIDED, RESOLUTION_ACCEPTED, RESOLUTION_REJECTED, ESCALATED or CLOSED); or a trade act GRN, CRAC, MSMED_OBJECTION or LEDGER_ACK. No other value is valid.
    items[], reason_code For kind: post_order only. On a RETURN, items[] (each {id, quantity}, at least one) and reason_code are REQUIRED. On a CANCEL, reason_code is REQUIRED and items[] is present only for a partial cancel. The gateway matches the write against them field by field (Presence proof). The summary names each item and quantity from the gateway's templates, and an item name appears only in counterparty_text[].
    subject_hash REQUIRED for the trade acts GRN, CRAC, MSMED_OBJECTION and LEDGER_ACK: the sha-256 of exactly what the principal is shown. For a document, the JCS hash of the exact body; for a ledger entry, its entry_hash; for a ledger statement, the head_hash at through_seq. For the issue actions RESOLUTION_ACCEPTED and RESOLUTION_REJECTED: the sha-256 over the JCS form of the issue's resolution as the gateway showed it in summary, absent when the issue has no resolution. Absent for every other action, including ESCALATED, INFO_PROVIDED and CLOSED. A trade-act render without subject_hash is invalid. The Trusted Surface shows how many times the resolution has been revised since the first step-up for this write. For ESCALATED, INFO_PROVIDED and CLOSED the Trusted Surface does not present a resolution as the thing confirmed; for CLOSED it states that closing does not accept a pending resolution.
    subject_ref REQUIRED for a trade act: {kind, id, through_seq?, payee?}. kind is order or document for a GRN, CRAC or MSMED_OBJECTION (with order_id), and ledger_entry (id is the entry id; payee REQUIRED for a payment entry in pending_ack with settlement_payee_unmatched) or ledger_statement (id is the ledger id, through_seq REQUIRED) for a LEDGER_ACK, which has no order_id. For a GRN the Trusted Surface shows the delivery date and the accepted and rejected quantities with their enum reasons; for an MSMED objection, the objection text labelled as counterparty text; for a ledger entry, its amount, type and due date and, for a payment entry in pending_ack with settlement_payee_unmatched, the payee of the cited receipt, labelled as not one of the creditor's settlement accounts; for a statement, the count, the closing balance, the oldest unpaid date and every entry above itemise_above, counting only the entries the other party posted.
    language The principal's language, as a BCP 47 tag (LanguageTag).
    checkout_hash The checkout_hash of the quote the step-up is about. For post_order it is the checkout_hash of the order's closed Checkout Mandate (the hash of the BPP-signed quote the order was placed under, also on the order's Receipt); an Order carries no checkout_hash of its own.
    total The total the principal pays, as a single figure (Amount, integer paise): the quote total plus the sum of the session's operator_charges[]. On a summary step-up, the sum of basket[].amount.
    charge_lines[] Optional. On a step-up that pays for an order, one entry {code, amount, recipient?} for each fee line, then one remainder entry for everything else. The amounts add up to total. Absent when the order has no fee line. The Trusted Surface reads them by TS-3 from its own templates.
    price_breakup_hash The hash of the BPP-signed price breakup and disclosures (disclosures.hash).
    seller_name The payee seller, from the BPP-signed quote. Absent on a summary step-up, whose sellers are in basket[].
    assisted_operators[] On an assisted session only: the participant_id and ref of each assisted_operator in the session's participants[].
    summary Gateway-written text (LangText) from the gateway's own templates, with no counterparty text in it.
    counterparty_text[] Optional. Each entry is {label, text}: a seller-supplied string (an item name, a fee label), labelled by the gateway.
    basket[] On a summary step-up over a multi-seller basket only: one line per seller, {bpp_id, payee_name, amount, handler, needs_pin, funds_locked, expires_at, funding}. payee_name is the name the payer's bank returns for the payee VPA. funds_locked and expires_at are the amount a block reserves and when it is released. funding is deposit (the principal's own money) or credit.
    covers[] On a summary step-up only: the legs the approval covers, each {session_id, checkout_hash, payee, amount} (Step-up). A summary's session_id and checkout_hash are those of the session whose complete raised it.
    catalogue_disclosure[] Optional. On a session step-up (confirm or rail_auth, including a summary step-up) only: one entry for each line item whose Disclosures.catalogue[] entry is sponsored or undeclared, or carries a prior_price. Each entry is {index, sponsorship, name_ref} and, for a prior price, prior_price and unit_price (the discovered unit price shown with it). index is the position of the entry in that session's Disclosures.catalogue[]; on a summary step-up each entry also carries cover_index, the position in covers[] of the leg whose session it is. A cover_index that is not a position in covers[], or an index that is not a position in that leg's Disclosures.catalogue[], makes the render invalid (evidence_unverifiable). name_ref is the position of the item's name in counterparty_text[]. An entry for an item that is not_sponsored and has no prior_price is invalid. Never present on a post_order step-up or a negotiation confirm. It is hashed into Presentation.content_hash with the rest of the render (Catalogue disclosure on the Trusted Surface).
    unrequested_lines[] On a session step-up (confirm or rail_auth) only: one entry {index, added_by, name_ref} for each line of the session whose added_by is seller or assisted_operator, and for each line whose added_by is agent that the agent has not marked principal_requested. index is the position of the line in the session's line_items[], and name_ref is the position of its name in counterparty_text[]. It is present only when it has an entry, and then it is also named in render_required[], so that a Trusted Surface that does not understand it renders nothing. The Trusted Surface reads every entry in full, before the total (Line provenance).
    requested_line_count Optional, on a session step-up. The number of lines that the agent marked principal_requested and that are not in unrequested_lines[]. The Trusted Surface counts them aloud ("and 11 items you asked for") and does not read them one by one unless the principal asks.
    plan On a session step-up (confirm or rail_auth) over a payment plan only: {plan_hash, tenders[]}, each tender {tender_id, handler, amount, sequence}. The Trusted Surface shows the whole plan, every tender and its amount, never one tender alone, and reads the plan's total as one figure with its tenders beside it. When the session has operator charges, the render reads the plan total and then each operator charge, and total is their sum. Requires checkout_hash. It is hashed into Presentation.content_hash with the rest of the render.
    offer_hash On a negotiation kind: confirm step-up only: the offer_hash of the offer the principal is asked to approve (Step-up to the Trusted Surface). Never present with checkout_hash.
    acceptance_revocation On a negotiation kind: confirm step-up about a firm offer only: the offer's acceptance_revocation value, copied unchanged; in this version only waived occurs, because receivers refuse allowed_until_receipt. Present only with offer_hash. It is hashed into Presentation.content_hash with the rest of the render.
    revoke_route Where the principal revokes the mandate or cancels, as a URI.
    grievance_contact The agent operator's grievance officer contact.
    expires_at As in the step_up object.

    Match code (ts_match_code). A Trusted Surface and an agent MAY show a 4-digit code that both derive from the content_hash of the render: the first 4 bytes of the digest, as an unsigned big-endian integer, modulo 10000, written as 4 digits. The code is a derived display value, not a field of the render and not transmitted, and it is subject to these rules:

    • It is shown on an app or web delivery only. On IVR and upi123pay it is not read, so that the principal hears one number only, the voice challenge (Voice confirmation).
    • It is never a voice challenge, never a control and never evidence. No gateway or verifier relies on it.
    • The text beside it says "A matching code does not make a link safe; only your bank's own app is safe" (hi-IN: "कोड मेल खाने से लिंक सुरक्षित नहीं हो जाता; सिर्फ़ आपके बैंक का अपना ऐप सुरक्षित है।", which says the same in English). The wording is a draft, under review.
    • It is a MAY and not a SHOULD. A phishing page shows no code, so comparing two displays does not stop a phisher, and "the codes match, so it is safe" is a compromised agent's best phishing line. It helps against a look-alike page that an honest agent relays and against a mix-up between concurrent step-ups, and it adds nothing to the wire.

    Content rules for ts_render and every Trusted Surface template:

    1. The total is one figure, with the breakup reachable through its hash. Its fee lines are read separately (TS-3), and their amounts, with the remainder, add up to it.
    2. A revoke or cancel route and a grievance contact are always present. BACP requires the grievance contact in every case. From 13-05-2027 the DPDP Act, 2023, s.6(3) requires a request for consent to give the contact details of the Data Protection Officer or another person authorised to answer the Data Principal, and the DPDP Rules, 2025, r.3(c) and r.9 require the notice to describe how to exercise rights and complain, and the business contact information of that person to be published and given in every response (Legal time bands).
    3. No add-on is preselected, and no text creates urgency.
    4. Seller free text appears only in counterparty_text[].
    5. On a negotiation step-up that carries acceptance_revocation, the Trusted Surface shows the value only as the fixed consequence sentence below, in the principal's language, followed by the offer's cancellation terms, which the gateway states in summary. It never shows the token, and it never says or implies that the order cannot be cancelled: cancelling after the contract is formed follows the cancellation terms.
    6. On a step-up whose render carries catalogue_disclosure[], the Trusted Surface repeats what discovery showed, before the total, as below.     Negotiation on the Trusted Surface¶

    These rules apply to the negotiation renders and notices of Negotiation.

    • The acceptance choice. At mandate set-up the Trusted Surface presents the two values of process.accept_confirmation, ask and autonomous, with equal weight, and preselects neither (TS-2). A mandate-setup Presentation that shows either value preselected is refused with 403 ts_render_mismatch. The set-up also captures process.notice_route, the route by which the gateway tells the principal about each autonomous act through a channel the agent does not control. In a consumer session this is the explicit and affirmative action that Rule 4(9) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 asks for.
    • Notice of an autonomous act (a notice render: a gateway-signed compact JWS, not a TsRender and not a step-up; its payload schema is open, Q-105). The gateway builds the notice of agreed_by_agent and purchase_offered_by_agent from its own templates, in the principal's language, and signs it as a render. It is not a step-up: the principal approves nothing. It states the seller or buyer name, offeror_kind, the all-in total (₹ with Indian digit grouping, spoken in words on a voice surface), the quantity and unit, the current-stage cancellation charge, the stop route available in that state, revoke_route, grievance_contact and its content_hash. It carries no URI other than those routes and no urgency text (TS-4), and never reaches the principal through the agent's output, where counterparty text could imitate it. The render and the receipt of its delivery go into the bundle as notice_renders[].
    • Decline of an AGREED or BOUND deal. The render shows the current-stage charge under the agreed cancellation.terms before the factor is taken, and the principal's confirmation is posted as the distinct event negotiation.agreed.declined, never negotiation.step_up.declined: one verb with two consequences cannot be told apart on a voice channel. The signed event with its Presentation is the proof of presence for the decline; no agent carries an hp_proof for it (Presence proof).
    • Offer that ran out. When an offer lapses during approval, or the acceptor's gateway refuses to dispatch within 5 seconds of expires_at, the Trusted Surface says that the offer ran out while the principal was approving, never that the principal declined. The hi-IN sentence says, in English, "The offer ran out while you were approving": "आपकी मंज़ूरी पूरी होने से पहले ही ऑफ़र का समय ख़त्म हो गया।" (offer_expired_during_approval).
    • IST. Every time that the Trusted Surface shows or tells a person is in IST, in the person's language. This is a MUST. It is separate from the machine values in signed bytes, which SHOULD carry +05:30.
    • Contested crossing. Each principal is asked on their own surface in one plain question, whether to proceed with the deal, with the same weight for each answer and neither preselected. Declining and silence have the same effect.

    Catalogue disclosure on the Trusted Surface¶

    An agent tells the principal about sponsored results and prior prices when it presents them (Disclosure phrases). The Trusted Surface says it again before the principal confirms, in words the agent does not control. For each entry of catalogue_disclosure[], in order, the Trusted Surface:

    1. shows or speaks the item's name as labelled seller text, taken from counterparty_text[name_ref] (TS-1);
    2. gives the sponsorship sentence for the entry: ts_sponsored for sponsored, ts_undeclared for undeclared, and no sentence for not_sponsored;
    3. if prior_price is present, gives the ts_prior_price sentence.

    All of this comes before the total, and under TS-3 audio is read in full before the principal enters the factor. When more than three entries are undeclared and carry no prior_price, the Trusted Surface MAY give one ts_undeclared_n sentence for them instead of their per-item sentences. It never paraphrases. A Trusted Surface in another language uses a fixed translation of the same sentence from its own templates. The wording is a draft, under review with the disclosure phrases, and its vocabulary is theirs, so that the principal hears the same words twice.

    Rules:

    • The gateway MUST include catalogue_disclosure[] on every session step-up of kind confirm or rail_auth, and on a summary step-up (with entries for every leg), whenever any qualifying item exists. It MUST NOT include it on a post_order step-up or on a negotiation confirm, which carries offer_hash and not checkout_hash.
    • The ts_render stays gateway-signed with the BAP registry key. The gateway MUST copy sponsorship, prior_price and unit_price unaltered from the session's Disclosures.catalogue[] entry (unit_price is its discovered_unit_price).
    • The Trusted Surface takes catalogue_disclosure[] only from the render, never from the agent. It does not verify seller signatures, so the repeat is only as trustworthy as the gateway's signature on it; a rogue gateway is caught on dispute (Evidence, step 6), and the agent checks it at run time (rule below).
    • An agent that receives a session step_up carrying a ts_render SHOULD check, before it hands the principal the trusted_surface_url, that catalogue_disclosure[] matches the session's Disclosures.catalogue[] by the evidence step 6 rule. On a mismatch it MUST NOT hand the link over, and it records discovery_source_mismatch.
    • A Trusted Surface on an earlier version ignores a field it does not know and would ask for the factor without the repeat. The gateway therefore MUST refuse a step_up.approved for a render that carries catalogue_disclosure[] when presentation.renders does not contain catalogue_disclosure (400 invalid_request, nothing changes).
    • A step-up exists only when a threshold, a new seller or another trigger applies. A session that never needs one never reaches a Trusted Surface, so the repeat is absent from it by construction, and the agent's own sentences and the type: disclosure messages (Message object) are then the only disclosure.
    • The item names and positions in catalogue_disclosure[] tell a payer PSP, on a rail_auth render, what was bought, beyond the base render. Whether the names of items should reach the payer PSP at all is open (Q-73).

    Presentation record¶

    Presentation (common.json $defs/Presentation) records what the Trusted Surface showed, how, and what the principal did:

    Field Required Meaning
    surface yes The subscriber id of the PSP or Mandate Manager whose surface presented it, equal to the subscriber id of the registry key that signed the event. Never the agent. The gateway refuses an event whose surface is not the signer's subscriber id with 404 not_found, and nothing changes.
    channel yes Channel (app, ivr, upi123pay, assisted, …).
    language yes LanguageTag, for example hi-IN. A language name such as "Hindi" is invalid.
    modality yes visual, audio or both.
    content_hash yes For a step-up, the sha-256 of the compact JWS ts_render. Otherwise the hash of the content the Trusted Surface rendered from its templates (for a mandate approval, the notice whose hash the ConsentReceipt carries in notice.hash).
    content_ref yes Where a party entitled to the evidence fetches that content to recompute the hash. For a step-up, it is an RFC 3986 fragment that holds an RFC 6901 JSON Pointer into the evidence bundle, #/ts_renders/<index>, where <index> is the zero-based position of the render in the bundle's ts_renders[], written without leading zeros. The bundle is signed and immutable, so the index is stable. It never points at the Mandate Manager and PSP only GET /trusted-surface/step-ups/{step_up_id}. For any other content it is an absolute URI.
    user_action yes tap_confirm, upi_pin, biometric, passkey, voice_confirm_plus_device or decline. biometric is the principal's own biometric enrolled for the principal on the Trusted Surface device, never an operator's.
    shown_at, acted_at yes When it was shown and when the principal acted.
    template_id no The Trusted Surface template and its version.
    renders on step_up.approved when the render carries catalogue_disclosure[] or plan, and on any step-up approval whose render lists a name in render_required[] The render fields beyond this version's base set that the Trusted Surface presented: catalogue_disclosure, plan, items, unrequested_lines or acceptance_revocation, for example ["catalogue_disclosure", "plan"]. It is the only extension point of Presentation.
    checkout_hash on step_up.approved The quote the content showed: the session's quote, or for a post_order step-up the checkout_hash of the order's closed Checkout Mandate. Never present with offer_hash. A post_order step-up for an order-less ledger act has no quote, and its approval carries none.
    offer_hash on negotiation.step_up.approved The offer a negotiation confirm step-up showed. Never present with checkout_hash.
    plan_hash on step_up.approved when the render carries plan The plan.plan_hash of the payment plan the content showed.
    device_signature no A JWS by a device key of the Trusted Surface over the JCS form of {step_up_id, content_hash, acted_at} (for a step-up) or {content_hash, acted_at}; its protected header carries the public key as jwk. Where the mandate lists principal.device_jkt[], it MUST verify against a key whose RFC 7638 thumbprint is listed, or the gateway answers 403 presence_not_proven. Where the mandate lists none, no verifier relies on it. It is never called an electronic signature under the Information Technology Act, 2000: whether it is an authentication technique under s.3A is not verified as of 18-10-2026 (Q-86).
    voice_evidence no Audio and transcript hashes and the translation model, for a voice confirmation.
    assisted_by on assisted The participant_id of the assisted operator, taken by the Trusted Surface from ts_render.assisted_operators[], never from the agent.

    A TrustedSurfaceEvent of type step_up.approved, negotiation.step_up.approved, mandate.activated, loan.accepted or payment.afa_completed MUST carry presentation. Without it the gateway refuses the event with 400 invalid_request and nothing changes. On step_up.approved the presentation MUST carry checkout_hash, except on the approval of a post_order step-up for an order-less ledger act (LEDGER_ACK with no order_id), which carries none; on negotiation.step_up.approved, the approval of a negotiation confirm step-up, it MUST carry offer_hash; it never carries both. On either approval its user_action MUST NOT be decline. The gateway refuses an event that breaks one of these rules with 400 invalid_request and nothing changes; this includes a step_up.approved posted for a negotiation step-up. A decline is posted only as step_up.declined, which SHOULD carry a Presentation with user_action: decline. A ConsentReceipt MAY carry the same record in captured_via.presentation.

    When the gateway receives a step_up.approved or negotiation.step_up.approved event it MUST check, before any state change:

    • that the event is signed by the step-up's Mandate Manager or payer PSP (Step-up signers); any other registry key gets 404 not_found, never 403, and nothing changes;
    • that presentation.surface is the subscriber id of the signing key, or refuse the event with 404 not_found (nothing changes);
    • that presentation.content_hash is the sha-256 of that step-up's ts_render, or refuse the event with 403 ts_render_mismatch;
    • on step_up.approved, that presentation.checkout_hash equals the session's current checkout_hash (for a post_order step-up, the checkout_hash of the order's closed Checkout Mandate; none for an order-less ledger act); an approval for an earlier quote approves nothing (Deterministic evaluation, step 8);
    • on negotiation.step_up.approved, that presentation.offer_hash equals the ts_render's offer_hash and the hash of the offer the step-up names, and, for a firm offer, that the ts_render's acceptance_revocation equals that offer's value; otherwise it refuses the event with 403 ts_render_mismatch, and nothing is sent;
    • on step_up.approved for a ts_render that carries catalogue_disclosure[], that presentation.renders contains catalogue_disclosure; otherwise it refuses the event with 400 invalid_request and nothing changes;
    • on a step-up approval for a ts_render that carries render_required[], that presentation.renders contains every name listed there; otherwise it refuses the event with 400 invalid_request and nothing changes;
    • on step_up.approved for a ts_render that carries plan, that presentation.renders contains plan (otherwise 400 invalid_request, nothing changes) and that presentation.plan_hash equals the render's plan.plan_hash (otherwise 403 ts_render_mismatch);
    • where the mandate carries a confirmation_floor, that presentation.user_action is not below its min_user_action and presentation.channel is in its channels_allow; otherwise it refuses the event with 403 presence_not_proven at $.presentation.user_action or $.presentation.channel and nothing changes;
    • where the mandate lists principal.device_jkt[], that presentation.device_signature verifies against a key whose thumbprint is listed; otherwise it refuses the event with 403 presence_not_proven and nothing changes;
    • that presentation.acted_at is not after the step-up's expires_at, or treat the step-up as expired (step_up_expired, no money moves);
    • on an assisted session, the assisted channel rules.

    While the session is in human_handoff the gateway holds a step_up.approved event that passes these checks and does not apply it. After the handoff is released it applies a held approval only if the step-up has not expired and its checkout_hash still equals the session's (Human handoff). For a summary step-up the re-check is per leg: a held approval applies to a released session only if that session's covers[] entry still equals its current checkout_hash, payee and amount.

    Voice confirmation¶

    user_action: voice_confirm_plus_device is a confirmation by voice or keypad on a call. Here "device" means the number or call leg that the Trusted Surface holds for the principal or payer, not a device key. The rules:

    • The Trusted Surface draws the challenge. It draws a challenge of at least 4 digits from its own random source, on its own call leg, single-use, for one step-up. The challenge is never in ts_render, StepUp, StepUpStatus or anything else that an agent sees, because an agent that bridges the call would know it. No field of the render derives it.
    • The answer is bound in the event. voice_evidence.challenge_hash is the sha-256 over the JCS form of {step_up_id, content_hash, challenge}, and is REQUIRED with this user_action. The gateway cannot recompute it: it is evidence, and the Trusted Surface's draw of an unpredictable value is a Trusted Surface conformance case, not a gateway check. A challenge that is guessed after the call from the hash is harmless, because it is single-use and ends with the call.
    • Keypad equals speech. The principal may answer by DTMF or by speech, with the same steps and the same weight, so that a feature phone keeps its path. No rule depends on speech recognition of the challenge.
    • Which calls count. Voice counts only on a call that the Trusted Surface placed to the number it holds for the principal or payer. It also counts when the principal dialled the Trusted Surface's published number from that number, but only together with the Trusted Surface's own challenge, because caller identity can be spoofed. The number is held by the Trusted Surface and is never in the mandate or the render.
    • Never money. The gateway refuses voice_confirm_plus_device on a rail_auth step-up, and on any confirm step-up whose approval releases a debit, with 403 presence_not_proven at $.presentation.user_action. Voice confirms only what moves no money (a cancel, an issue acceptance). RBI/2025-26/79 paras 5(a) and 6(b) and UPI Consolidated Circular v1.0 para 19.2.2 settle this for money (Q-13). In human-not-present (HNP) the debit rests on the mandate's authentication model, so the refusal there is BACP policy implementing Q-13, not an RBI requirement. A PIN entered over a UPI 123PAY IVR stays upi_pin.
    • No PIN on a Trusted Surface call. A call that the Trusted Surface places never takes a UPI PIN (PIN rule).
    • Agent side. An agent's own voice channel MUST NOT stay on, conference or bridge the call leg of the Trusted Surface. This is an agent conformance disclosure check and cannot be observed on the wire.

    Multi-seller basket on the Trusted Surface¶

    A multi-seller basket reaches the principal as one summary step-up (reason rail_fallback) whose ts_render lists every seller in basket[]. The Trusted Surface:

    • states the number of sellers first, and for a human-present plan the number of payments and of PINs needed (for example "3 sellers, 3 payments, 3 UPI PINs"), before any line;
    • then shows or reads each basket[] line: the bank-returned payee name, the amount, whether a PIN is needed, for a block the amount locked and until when, and whether the money is the principal's own (deposit) or credit; under TS-3, audio is read in full before the principal enters any factor;
    • for a block whose debits the gateway cannot see, gives the unseen-draw statement that the ts_render carries in summary: that this seller can draw up to the stated amount unseen by BACP and can change the block's amount or validity (Usage ledger). The same statement is in the notice when such a block is set up (Issuance, step 4);
    • for a block whose mandate is paused for unmatched_rail_debit, shows the route to revoke the block in the UPI app (NPCI UPI Consolidated Circular v1.0, para 16.10), because the pause stops BACP payments but not draws at the rail;
    • shows the summary as approval of a plan, never as a payment: each human-present leg is then a separate step-up with its own ts_render, its own content_hash and the principal's own factor, and a summary Presentation alone authorises no debit.

    A new per-seller block (3b in the per-leg rule) is offered only beside a one-time human-present payment to the same seller, with equal weight: the same number of steps, neither option preselected, and declining the block no harder than paying once (TS-2). It is never offered after a failed payment, and never on a step-up with delivery: ivr_callback. On ivr_callback and UPI 123PAY every leg is a human-present call of its own, read in full before its PIN.

    Assisted channel¶

    The following is BACP policy. No law found requires it; it exists so that an assisted operator is visible in the evidence and cannot approve for the principal.

    • A session is assisted when its channel is assisted or the agent's AgentCredential has agent.class: assisted. An assisted agent that declares channel: app is still bound by every rule below.
    • An assisted session MUST list an assisted_operator in participants[]. Otherwise POST /sessions returns 400 invalid_request at $.participants.
    • Every Trusted Surface approval on an assisted session MUST name the operator in presentation.assisted_by. The Trusted Surface takes it from ts_render.assisted_operators[], which the gateway builds from the session's participants[], never from the agent. The gateway refuses an assisted_by that is not an assisted_operator of the session with 403 presence_not_proven.
    • The principal approves with the principal's own factor: user_action is upi_pin or biometric, never a tap. biometric means the principal's own biometric enrolled for the principal on the Trusted Surface device. The gateway refuses any other approval with 403 presence_not_proven (for example a schema-valid presentation.channel: app approval on an assisted session), and the session stays requires_escalation.
    • A Presentation that fails its schema (for example channel: assisted with user_action: tap_confirm) is refused with 400 invalid_request, as for every schema failure, and nothing changes.
    • The operator may read the content aloud (modality: audio or both); the factor is the principal's.
    • An assisted operator who reads discovery results aloud uses the same fixed sentences as an agent (Disclosure phrases).

    assisted_by and Participant.ref are attribution, never authentication. A ref is namespaced scheme:id, where scheme is csc_vle, bank_bc, ondc_np_agent or other, and the gateway sets Participant.ref_verification to asserted or verified. The gateway writes verified only when it can name the verifying registry, and none exists today (Q-62), so asserted is the only value in practice. An agent that supplies verified gets 400 invalid_request. The Trusted Surface does not read "unverified" aloud (content rule 7); the evidence summary and the bundle flag the reference as asserted.

    The Information Technology Act, 2000, s.11(b) attributes an electronic record to the originator when it is sent "by a person who had the authority to act on behalf of the originator in respect of that electronic record", and s.11(c) when it is sent "by an information system programmed by or on behalf of the originator to operate automatically" (India Code text). Whether an operator's tap could be attributed to the principal under either is a question for counsel (Q-86), and whether voice, tap or operator-relayed confirmation is valid consent is open (Q-13). Who certifies assisted operators is open (Q-62).

    StepUp.delivery: assisted gives the step-up at least 5 minutes before expires_at (and at most 15). The 5-minute floor is a BACP design value, not an external requirement.

    AI disclosure¶

    The agent's first message in every session is the AI disclosure, with typed routes to a human (Sessions and Checkout):

    • Every session carries ai_disclosure{notice, handoff_routes[]}, taken from the AgentCredential's disclosure.ai_notice and disclosure.handoff_routes. ai_disclosure is always present; handoff_routes is absent when the credential lists none. The disclosure message points at it with param: $.ai_disclosure.
    • An AgentCredential whose agent.channels includes voice, ivr or upi123pay MUST list a tel: route (RFC 3966), optionally with DTMF digits (for example "press 0"), because a feature-phone caller cannot open a web link. A route with DTMF digits is always a tel: route. The schema rejects a credential that lists only https: routes, and a route that pairs DTMF digits with an https: URI.
    • An agent SHOULD NOT split one intended order into several payments to change the fee that a seller bears. The gateway's suspected-split-order check enforces it (Split-order detection); BACP adds no new trigger for it.
    • The gateway writes an InstructionLog entry at stage ai_disclosure, actor: agent. It is the first entry whose actor is agent, before intent. The stage disclosure is the seller's quote, not the AI disclosure.
    • On the network, context.bacp.ai_disclosure: true is REQUIRED, and the gateway MUST mirror it into the bacp tag group, so that a seller learns that an AI agent is ordering even when a validator strips context.bacp (Beckn / ONDC Binding). The gateway MAY also send acting_for, operator_name and operator_grievance_contact. It sends acting_for.gstin and acting_for.display_name only when the mandate's data_scopes include ACTING_FOR, and otherwise omits them.

    Human handoff¶

    A principal can ask for a human at any time, including after the mandate is revoked.

    1. The agent calls POST /sessions/{session_id}/handoff (tag bacp-browse) with action: request and one of the session's ai_disclosure.handoff_routes (no route when the session lists none). The agent picks a route whose languages include the session's language, where one exists, and otherwise says so to the principal. The gateway MUST accept it in every session state and whatever the mandate's status, and returns 201.
    2. An open session moves to status human_handoff. While it is there, the gateway refuses PUT and complete from the agent with 409 session_state_conflict at $.status, and holds any step_up.approved event without applying it (Presentation record). A session in any other state keeps its status and records handoff.
    3. A human support operator accepts with action: accept, signed with a registry key of role support_operator under tag bacp-handoff. Only then does the gateway set handoff.accepted_by (that key's id) and handoff.accepted_at. The gateway reads the key's registry record, which names the agent operator (operator_id) and the one person who holds the key, and answers an acceptance as follows, each time without changing the handoff:
      • a key whose registry role is not support_operator, or a signature under any other tag: 401 signature_invalid;
      • a support_operator key of another agent operator than the session's: 404 not_found, so that the key has no standing and the answer is no oracle for which sessions exist;
      • an accept while accepted_by is already set: 409 session_state_conflict at $.handoff.accepted_by.
    4. The handoff ends with action: release. The gateway accepts a release signed under bacp-handoff by the accepted_by key, or signed by the agent under bacp-browse only while accepted_by is absent (withdrawing an unanswered request). A release leaves the session in human_handoff, changes nothing and is answered as follows: a key whose role is not support_operator: 401 signature_invalid; another agent operator's support_operator key: 404 not_found; a key of the same agent operator that is not accepted_by, or an agent's release after acceptance: 409 session_state_conflict at $.handoff.accepted_by. On release a session in human_handoff returns to incomplete, the gateway sets handoff.ended_at, and then applies a held approval only if its step-up has not expired and its checkout_hash still equals the session's.
    5. The operator binding in steps 3 and 4 and this restart ship together, because each restart is a fresh chance for a competitor's key to accept first. accept and release are each one compare-and-set on handoff.accepted_by and handoff.ended_at, so that they are linearisable on any instances. An accept sets accepted_by only while both are absent. An agent's release sets ended_at only while accepted_by is still absent; if an accept won, the release changes nothing. An accept after ended_at is set gets 409 session_state_conflict at $.status and changes nothing. A request that arrives while ended_at is set starts a new handoff in one compare-and-set that applies only while ended_at is still set, in which the gateway clears accepted_by, accepted_at and ended_at, sets requested_at and route from the request, returns 201, and the earlier handoff stays in the InstructionLog at stage handoff. An accept is therefore refused only for the handoff that has ended, never for one requested afterwards. A session therefore never ends incomplete with accepted_by set.
    6. The gateway writes every act to the InstructionLog at stage handoff, an acceptance or a release by a support operator with actor: gateway and the operator's key id in actor_ref.

    Session.handoff is {route, requested_at, accepted_by, accepted_at, ended_at}. A handoff is requested while accepted_by is absent, connected once it is set, and ended once ended_at is set. A new request after a handoff has ended starts the next handoff of the session (Human handoff, step 5). A session in human_handoff leaves it by a release (to incomplete) or by cancel, TTL or a stopped mandate (to cancelled). On the network a handoff maps to Beckn support → on_support, and the MCP tool is bacp_human_handoff (MCP and A2A).

    Presence proof¶

    presence: HP on a post-order write (cancel, return, raise issue, issue action) or on a trade act (GRN, CRAC, MSMED objection, ledger acknowledgement) is not only a declaration. The principal proves it on the Trusted Surface, through a step-up of kind post_order (Presence, Post-order gate):

    1. A post-order write with presence: HP and no hp_proof gets 403 presence_not_proven. The error carries step_up, a StepUp with kind: post_order and reason: presence_proof, whose ts_render names the write: action, order_id and, for an issue action, issue_id and, for RESOLUTION_ACCEPTED or RESOLUTION_REJECTED, subject_hash; for a trade act, subject_ref and subject_hash; for a RETURN, items and reason_code, and for a CANCEL, reason_code. Nothing is sent to the network. The gateway keeps at most one pending post_order step-up per (subject_ref, action) (for an order write, per (order_id, action, issue_id)): a step-up is pending until it expires, is declined, or its proof is consumed. A repeat of the refused write returns the same step_up_id and ts_render, except that for RESOLUTION_ACCEPTED or RESOLUTION_REJECTED on an issue whose resolution no longer hashes to the pending step-up's subject_hash the gateway voids that step-up and creates a new one. A voided step-up is no longer pending: the gateway refuses every event for it with 404 not_found and nothing changes, and serves its ts_render to no one. The creation of post_order step-ups is limited per (agent, principal) and per order_id, a replacement for a step-up voided because the counterparty revised the resolution is limited per (principal, issue_id) instead, and a principal who declined is not asked again at once (Limits on step-ups). A step-up reaches its final state through the status read described in step 3.
    2. The principal confirms on the Trusted Surface, which posts the usual step_up.approved event with its Presentation. The gateway checks it as for any step-up approval.
    3. The agent retries the write with hp_proof{ts_event_hash, step_up_id}: the post_order step-up's id and the sha-256 over the JCS form of its signed step_up.approved event. Both fields are REQUIRED. The retry uses a new Idempotency-Key: the refused request and the retry differ in body, so the same key gets 422 idempotency_conflict, whose resolution is recoverable and whose message names a new key (Idempotency). The agent learns the ts_event_hash from the step-up status, never from the Trusted Surface: GET /step-ups/{step_up_id} (tag bacp-browse, a different path from the render fetch of the Mandate Manager and PSP) returns StepUpStatus {step_up_id, kind, status, ts_event_hash?}, where status is pending, approved, declined, expired, voided or superseded, and ts_event_hash is present exactly when status is approved. The step_up.updated webhook carries the same object. Only the agent credential whose request created the step-up may read it: any other caller gets 404 not_found, including another credential of the same agent operator. A step-up the gateway voided after a counterparty's revision reads voided, and a write that cites it gets 403 presence_not_proven with a new Error.step_up.
    4. The gateway accepts the proof only for the action, order_id and issue_id named in that step-up's ts_render. A proof issued for another order, action or issue gets 403 presence_not_proven. For a RETURN it also matches the write field by field against the ts_render: the items, as a multiset of {id, quantity}, and the reason_code (a CANCEL matches its reason_code, and its items where the render carries them). A write that differs gets 403 presence_not_proven and sends nothing to the network. No new hash travels on the wire: the render is signed, so content_hash already covers both fields. The gateway's pending key for a RETURN adds a digest of {items sorted by id, reason_code}, so that separate returns of milk and of atta do not collide; the digest is not a wire field. The gateway consumes a RETURN proof only if no other approved RETURN proof of the same order overlaps it in items; a second, overlapping proof gets 403 presence_not_proven. While a RETURN step-up of an order is pending, the gateway creates no second RETURN step-up of that order whose items overlap it: the repeat returns the existing one. For RESOLUTION_ACCEPTED and RESOLUTION_REJECTED the gateway also hashes the issue's current resolution when the write arrives and refuses the proof with 403 presence_not_proven if that hash is not the ts_render's subject_hash (or if a resolution exists where none was shown): a remedy the seller revised after the principal confirmed needs a new confirmation. Any other issue action, in particular ESCALATED, is not bound to the resolution and is accepted on a valid proof for its own action. For a trade act the gateway hashes the submitted subject (the exact document body, the entry's entry_hash, or the head_hash at through_seq) and refuses the proof with 403 presence_not_proven, without consuming it, if that hash is not the ts_render's subject_hash: an act that differs from what the principal saw, for example a GRN with an objection added, needs a new confirmation. A statement acknowledgement whose head_hash is no longer the ledger's head is refused with 409 ledger_head_mismatch.
    5. An hp_proof is single-use. The gateway consumes it inside the atomic section keyed on the order's root Checkout Mandate (Usage ledger), or, for a trade act that has no order, keyed on the ledger_id, so that of two writes citing one proof, on any instances, exactly one succeeds and the other gets 403 presence_not_proven.

    A human-present complete is proven by the principal's signatures on the closed mandates and needs no hp_proof.

    Limits on step-ups¶

    The gateway publishes every figure below in its profile (step_up_limits in /.well-known/bacp), as a count over a window in the style of negotiation.step_up_cap. BACP sets no number: they are the gateway's own design values and are never presented as external limits. A gateway that declares none is tested against the limit that the conformance fixture sets.

    • Creation. The creation of post_order step-ups is counted per (agent, principal) and per order_id, and the creation of session step-ups is counted per (agent, principal) like post_order step-ups (session_per_principal). A replacement that the gateway creates after a counterparty's revision is not counted here. An excess gets 429 with Retry-After. One principal's exhausted quota never blocks another principal of the same agent, whose write still gets 403 presence_not_proven with a step_up.
    • Render fetch. The ts_render fetch is counted per caller key before the step-up is looked up, as before, and also per (caller key, step_up_id) after the standing check. An excess gets 429 with Retry-After.
    • Decline cool-off. Only an explicit step_up.declined starts it. Expiry does not, so a principal who was away has a way back (for the step-up itself, expiry still means decline, TS-2). For a post_order step-up the key is (order_id, action, issue_id), and for a session step-up it is (root open Checkout Mandate or, without one, principal, bpp_id, kind), so that opening a new session does not escape it. Inside the published window, a new step-up for that key is refused with 409 session_state_conflict and Retry-After set to the time left, with no new step-up and no prompt. There is no escape when the subject changes: a changed reason_code or quantity does not reopen the key, because an agent could then flip a field to dodge the cool-off. A principal who declined by mistake waits out the window or uses the grievance route. The Trusted Surface never asks the principal to confirm a decline, and the decline label is neutral (TS-2). The basis is the dark-pattern guideline on "nagging" (Annexure 1, item 10) and, from 01-01-2027, Rule 4(15) of the E-Commerce Rules.
    • Seller re-prompt cap. A revision of an issue's resolution voids the pending step-up and would create a replacement. Replacements are counted per (principal, issue_id) under the published limit. Past it, the gateway creates no new step-up, answers the agent 409 session_state_conflict, shows the principal no prompt, and the issue goes to the grievance route. Each render of a replacement carries the template sentence "the seller has changed this offer N times" (hi-IN: "विक्रेता ने यह ऑफ़र N बार बदला है।"; draft wording), never seller text. Without the cap a seller could revise again and again until a tired principal accepts a worse remedy. Past the cap a principal can no longer accept a later, better offer in the flow and uses the grievance route.

    Retry-After is REQUIRED on each of these 429 and 409 responses. The hi-IN sentence says, in English: "The seller has changed this offer N times."

    Seller-side and trade acts¶

    Where a money clock starts, the record says whether a human or an agent acted. A credit-ledger acknowledgement, a GRN, a CRAC and an MSMED written objection carry a typed actor{kind, role, participant_ref, mandate_ref} (common.json $defs/Actor; Trade Profiles). A human acts as principal, assisted_operator or seller_operator; an agent acts as agent or sub_agent.

    An agent records such an act only when the principal is present: the act MUST carry hp_proof of a post_order step-up whose ts_render names it (action GRN, CRAC, MSMED_OBJECTION or LEDGER_ACK, with subject_ref and subject_hash). No mandate scope authorises an agent's trade act, and mandate_ref on the actor only records the mandate the agent acted under (for an order-less ledger act it also names the Mandate Manager that issues the step-up).

    A gateway that lists in.bacp.trade_document (GRN, CRAC, MSMED objection) or in.bacp.ledger (ledger acknowledgement) offers one operation, POST /trade-acts (tag bacp-post-order), and, for documents, GET /orders/{order_id}/documents:

    1. The agent posts the act with presence: HP and no hp_proof. presence: HNP is refused with 403 presence_not_allowed.
    2. The gateway answers 403 presence_not_proven with a post_order step-up whose ts_render carries action, subject_ref and subject_hash. Nothing is sent to the seller and nothing is appended to a ledger.
    3. The principal approves on the Trusted Surface, which shows exactly the subject, and the agent retries with hp_proof and a new Idempotency-Key (Presence proof).
    4. The response carries, for a GRN, a CRAC or an MSMED objection, delivery: recorded or delivery: sent.

    Recorded, not sent. Until the carriage of a buyer-issued GRN or CRAC on Beckn is settled (Q-33), delivery is recorded: the gateway MUST NOT emit a Beckn GRN or CRAC for a recorded act, a recorded act MUST NOT set or move the MSMED due date in the BACP ledger, and support staff see it as "not sent to seller". A principal who files an objection is told on the Trusted Surface that it is recorded but not yet sent to the seller, and that it does not stop the 15-day clock of the MSMED Act until it is sent in writing (whether an objection sent by an agent is in writing, and whether a recorded objection starts or stops the clock, is open: Q-21, Q-83). A ledger acknowledgement is BACP-native, takes effect at once and carries no delivery.

    The documents' bodies carry counterparty text (a reason, an objection). The Trusted Surface labels it as counterparty text, and an agent MUST NOT load a counterparty DocumentRef.uri into model context.

    Fail-safe defaults¶

    Every open question marked "Open, fail-safe default" on Open Questions has a default that refuses, escalates to the principal on the Trusted Surface, or keeps a feature off. Such a default is relaxed only in a dated release of BACP:

    • A gateway, Mandate Manager or PSP MUST NOT apply a relaxed form of a fail-safe default by configuration, profile field or bilateral agreement while it claims a conformance level of a release that states the default. No profile field signals a relaxed default; the profile is fetched unsigned and could not prove one.
    • An escalation to the Trusted Surface under a fail-safe default (Q-12, Q-52) leaves a signed Trusted Surface event with a Presentation in the evidence bundle.
    • Conformance cases T1-27 to T1-32 test the six fail-safe defaults of 2026-10-18 (Human-participant cases).

    Evidence summary profile (informative)¶

    Dispute staff, a grievance officer, PSP support or the National Consumer Helpline need to read a bundle, often in the complainant's language. The profile in.bacp.evidence.summary.1 is an informative, human-readable rendering of an evidence bundle:

    • It is built only from the signed artefacts in the bundle (mandates, the BPP-signed quote, receipts, Trusted Surface events), never from operator-written summaries.
    • It is deterministic and template-built: the same bundle, language and template version give identical bytes. Amounts are in ₹ with Indian digit grouping, times in IST, and each item carries a flag saying whether its signature verified.
    • It carries the bundle_hash (sha-256 over the JCS form of the bundle) that it summarises.
    • For a negotiation it states the outcome of a crossing in one sentence, in English and hi-IN, built from signed artefacts alone (four integers: the two sender_log_seq and the two log_seq):
      • withdraw won: "The seller's withdrawal reached the buyer's system before the buyer sent the acceptance, so there is no agreement." / "ख़रीदार के मंज़ूरी भेजने से पहले ही, विक्रेता का ऑफ़र वापस लेने का संदेश ख़रीदार के सिस्टम तक पहुँच गया, इसलिए कोई सौदा नहीं हुआ।"
      • accept won: "The buyer's acceptance reached the seller's system before the seller sent the withdrawal, so the agreement stands." / "विक्रेता के ऑफ़र वापस लेने का संदेश भेजने से पहले ही, ख़रीदार की मंज़ूरी विक्रेता के सिस्टम तक पहुँच गई, इसलिए सौदा पक्का है।"
      • contested: "The order and the withdrawal crossed: confirm or cancel. No breach is recorded either way." / "ऑर्डर और ऑफ़र वापस लेने का संदेश, दोनों एक-दूसरे के पहुँचने से पहले ही भेजे गए: पुष्टि करें या रद्द करें। आप जो भी चुनें, किसी पर सौदा तोड़ने का मामला दर्ज नहीं होता।"
      • impossible: "Both systems show they received the other's message first, so the record cannot be relied on and is treated as contested." / "दोनों सिस्टम दिखाते हैं कि दूसरे का संदेश उन्हें पहले मिला, इसलिए इस रिकॉर्ड पर भरोसा नहीं किया जा सकता और इसे विवादित माना जाता है।"
    • It is never evidence. A reviewer verifies the bundle, not the summary.

    Requesting a summary. GET /evidence/{transaction_id}/summary?language= (tag bacp-browse; MCP tool bacp_evidence_summary_get with the arguments transaction_id and language) returns EvidenceSummary: {profile, bundle_hash, language, template_id, text, items[]}, each item {ref, verified}. The callers are the callers of the bundle (Access), each served only the summary of the bundle built for it, so that a seller or a payer PSP gets no other seller's lines. The grievance officer named in the operator's grievance_contact may also fetch it, acting for the principal: the complainant's copy of the complaint, which G.S.R. 789(E) r.4(5) requires from 01-01-2027, then reaches a principal who has no API client. Anyone else gets 403 evidence_forbidden. A gateway MAY offer the endpoint; the summary is never evidence.
