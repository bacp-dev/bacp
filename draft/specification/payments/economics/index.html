    Who pays¶

    Version 2026-10-18

    This page is part of BACP version 2026-10-18. It is informative: it explains the rules that other pages make normative, and it states each fact with its binding status and an "as of" date. Where it says MUST or SHOULD, it repeats a rule from the page it links.

    BACP makes normative what the principal pays and to whom. It leaves informative how sellers, payment aggregators (PAs) and banks split their own costs. So BACP has no mdr, fee_bearer, payer or settlement_eta field, and none of the seller's own costs appears in a quote line, a total, a ts_render, a message to the principal or a receipt.

    The normative rules are in Fees on the payer's screen, Fee-bearer change, the handler intersection, Refunds, the Trusted Surface read-out, Ranking by payment rungs and Operator compensation.

    The charges¶

    "Binding" says what kind of text the rule comes from. A rate that is announced and not in force says so. Nothing on this page is a net figure for any party.

    Charge Who sets it Who bears it at the rail Who may re-allocate it Where it appears in BACP Binding source and status (as of 18-10-2026)

    The two texts on small-merchant (P2PM) status are quoted on Rail Bindings and not reconciled. The MDR on a refund is unknown: no public NPCI or RBI text found says whether MDR is reversed when a refund is made (not verified as of 18-10-2026). BACP's refund rule does not depend on the answer, and a refund is never reduced by MDR or PA fees.

    Settlement and the seller's terms¶

    BACP does not carry a settlement_eta. The seller's settlement terms are ONDC's settlement_basis, settlement_window and withholding_amount, carried unchanged. The PA marketplace limits (PA Master Direction paras 10(b) and 13(f)) stay open with RBI (Q-51).

    Money maps¶

    These maps show who receives money from the payer's money, with no net figure.

    A kirana counter, paid by UPI at the shop. The payer pays the shop's VPA. The merchant's acquiring bank and the PA, if any, take their costs from the merchant's side under their own agreements. BACP sees only the order total and the receipt's rrn and utr.

    An ONDC prepaid order. The buyer app or the seller app collects (collected_by). The payer pays one amount. Settlement between the collector, the seller and any logistics provider follows ONDC's settlement terms. Who bears the collector's P2M MDR when the buyer app collects is open with ONDC (Q-80); this reading is an analysis, not a published rule.

    A direct-to-consumer order with a return. The payer pays the total, including any disclosed charge lines. On a return the refund goes back to the original method. It equals the amount paid less only the lines that the terms disclosed as retained and that the Rules permit; a short refund gets refund_short_of_terms, and the money is not blocked.

    A B2B order on NEFT with net-30 terms. The payer pays on the due date by NEFT, outside any quote line. MDR does not apply. A credit-ledger entry or a term sheet states the terms (Credit and Split Tender).

    How a seller or an operator can game the lines¶

    These are the patterns the rules are written to catch.

    • Steering to MDR-free primitives. A seller or a gateway ranks sellers by whether they carry a rail with no MDR. The default ranking must not use rungs, MDR or the finder fee (Ranking by payment rungs).
    • Splitting. An agent splits one order into payments under ₹2,000 to change the fee a seller bears. An agent SHOULD NOT do it, and the suspected-split-order check applies.
    • Uniform-fee recovery after para 7.5. A seller that may not surcharge UPI charges the same convenience fee on every debit mode. That is lawful under para 7.5 when it is disclosed upfront; BACP does not warn on it, and the fee is in the total from round 1. A fee that first appears in a later round is drip pricing.
    • Mode-specific discounts. A discount for one mode is the mirror of a surcharge on the others. Whether it is a negative surcharge under para 7.5 is open with counsel.
    • Discount for lock. A discount offered only if the principal sets up a block or a mandate can nudge her to a rung that costs the seller less. BACP shows an offer with its condition (Discounts) and does not rule on whether such an offer is lawful.
    • Collector incidence. On a buyer-app-collected order, the party that collects may be the P2M merchant. Which party bears the MDR is open (Q-80).
    • Operator incentives. A recommender that is paid by the recommended seller is the main gaming risk. The operator discloses how it is paid in the mandate notice before the principal approves, an item whose selection is paid for is sponsored with by: agent, and the finder fee is never a quote line.

    What each human sees¶

    • A principal at a screen. The total, each fee line with who receives it, and the remaining figure as one amount, before the factor.
    • A principal on voice, IVR or UPI 123PAY. The same, read in her language from the Trusted Surface's own templates. A feature-phone or IVR principal never sees a payment page and cannot follow a hash, so each fee line is read one at a time. An order with no fee line adds no audio.
    • A principal whose agent pays on its own (HNP). She agreed to the operator's compensation at mandate approval and, if she set convenience_fees, to a convenience fee up to her cap. Anything else raises a confirm step-up first.
    • A seller. A seller's quote has no operator line: the operator's fee sits outside the BPP quote.

    Informative and normative¶

    Every statement above about an NPCI FAQ is about an FAQ, which is not a circular. A statement about a rate in the future says "announced, not in force" until a date is notified. This page is re-checked at each release against the sources, and its dated facts (the MDR start, RBI/2017-18/105, the passage of Bill No. 150 of 2026) are re-verified then.
