    Glossary¶

    Version 2026-10-18

    This page is part of BACP version 2026-10-18. Rules marked Proposed depend on an open question and may change in a later dated release.

    This glossary defines the terms used across the BACP specification. Where a term is defined normatively on another page, the entry links to it. Terms are grouped by topic and sorted alphabetically within each group.

    Ecosystem¶

    AA
    Account Aggregator. RBI's framework for consented data sharing, built on DEPA (the Data Empowerment and Protection Architecture). BACP mandates take their lifecycle from AA consent artefacts: immutable, purpose-coded, use-counted and revocable.
    AP2
    Agent Payments Protocol. BACP mandates are wire-compatible with AP2 open and closed Checkout and Payment Mandates.
    Beckn
    The open protocol on which ONDC runs: signed {context, message} requests, an ACK, and an asynchronous on_* callback for each action (search, select, init, confirm, status, cancel, update and others).
    DPDP Act
    The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and its Rules. It governs the personal data an agent shares.
    HP / HNP
    Human present / human not present. See Presence.
    NPCI
    National Payments Corporation of India, which operates UPI and sets its product rules and limits.
    ONDC
    Open Network for Digital Commerce, India's Beckn-based commerce network, with its registry, gateways, domain specifications, IGM and RSF.
    UCP
    Universal Commerce Protocol. BACP's checkout session follows UCP checkout's lifecycle and status names (BACP spells cancelled and adds human_handoff). BACP is not wire-compatible with UCP; the differences are listed in BACP and other protocols.
    UPI
    Unified Payments Interface, India's real-time interoperable payment system operated by NPCI.

    Parties and roles¶

    AFA
    Additional Factor of Authentication. The second factor that RBI rules require for digital payments, such as a UPI PIN or a card OTP. An agent is never an AFA. See Payments.
    Agent
    The AI software (an LLM runtime, voice or text) that acts for a principal. BACP treats the agent as untrusted: it proposes, and a deterministic engine decides.
    Agent operator
    The legal entity that runs an agent and holds its AgentCredential. It is identified in the credential by legal name and entity id (CIN, LLPIN, GSTIN or PAN), and it names a grievance officer. Also called the agent provider.
    Assisted operator
    A human intermediary, such as a village-level entrepreneur, business correspondent or kirana operator, who completes a transaction for a principal at a physical touchpoint. Sessions with one use channel: assisted. See Trade Profiles.
    BACP gateway
    The BACP endpoint that agents call. It is co-located with, or runs on behalf of, a registered BAP. It verifies signatures and credentials, runs the mandate verifier and the negotiation guard, translates calls into signed Beckn messages, and keeps the replay cache, idempotency store and reservation ledger.
    BAP
    Beckn Application Platform (the buyer app). The Beckn/ONDC network participant that acts for buyers. It is identified on the network by its subscriber id (bap_id). In BACP the BAP hosts the BACP gateway, so the BAP subscriber is the network-visible party for every agent order.
    BG
    Beckn Gateway. The network component that multicasts a search to sellers. It routes only and does not see personal data.
    BPP
    Beckn Provider Platform, or Seller App. The Beckn/ONDC network participant that acts for sellers: catalogue, quotes, fulfilment, cancellations, returns and grievance responses.
    Consent Manager
    Under the DPDP Act, a registered entity through which a Data Principal gives, manages and withdraws consent. The Mandate Manager acts as a Consent Manager only if it is registered with the Data Protection Board (Q-16).
    Data Fiduciary
    Under the DPDP Act, the entity that decides the purpose and means of processing personal data. Every seller that receives a buyer's personal data is a separate Data Fiduciary.
    Data Principal
    Under the DPDP Act, the individual to whom personal data relates. In BACP this is usually the principal.
    GRO
    Grievance Redressal Officer. The officer that each seller, seller app and buyer app appoints to handle consumer grievances.
    Mandate Manager
    The service that turns a principal's approval on the Trusted Surface into signed, immutable mandates and consent receipts. It keeps mandate status, usage and revocation, publishes a status list, and notifies counterparties of changes within 60 seconds. It is data-blind: it stores mandates and logs, never order or payment payloads. See Mandates and Consent.
    PA
    Payment Aggregator. An RBI-authorised entity that collects payments for merchants. Before money moves, the gateway checks that the party in collected_by is an authorised PA or a bank.
    Principal
    The person or business on whose behalf an agent acts. The principal sets up, pauses and revokes mandates and answers step-ups, always on the Trusted Surface.
    PSP
    Payment Service Provider. In UPI, the payer PSP is the app or bank through which the payer pays. In BACP the payer PSP creates standing authorities, signs rail-binding attestations, executes debits and refunds, and signs receipts.
    Registry
    The source of truth for network participants' keys. For BACP this is the ONDC registry or another accredited registry. It publishes subscriber keys for BAPs, BPPs, Mandate Managers and PSPs, issues AgentCredentials and their status lists, and records conformance attestations. See Discovery and Profiles.
    Trusted Surface
    A role, not an app: a non-agentic surface run by a payer PSP or a Mandate Manager (an app, an IVR callback or an assisted counter) on which the principal reads notices in their language, approves mandates and step-ups, and enters a PIN or biometric. No agent can drive it, read the PIN, or post its events. It renders a step-up only from the gateway-signed ts_render it fetches by step_up_id, meets requirements TS-1 to TS-6, and records a Presentation of what it showed. Events from the Trusted Surface reach the gateway only at POST /trusted-surface/events, signed under tag bacp-ts with the registry key of the step-up's Mandate Manager or payer PSP: for a session step-up, the PSP named in rail_binding.psp_id of the leg's planned open Payment Mandate under the session's root; for a summary step-up over a multi-seller basket, only the root's Mandate Manager; for a post_order step-up, the PSP named in auth_binding.psp_id of the order's closed Payment Mandate(s) (Step-up signers). See Human Participants and the Trusted Surface.

    Identity and signing¶

    AgentCredential
    An SD-JWT verifiable credential (vct: in.bacp.agent.1) that identifies an agent, its operator and its BAP, binds the agent's public key (cnf), lists the signature tags the key may use (purposes), and carries the AI disclosure and human-handoff link. Schema: agent-credential.json. See Identity and Request Signing.
    cnf
    The confirmation key claim (RFC 7800). In an AgentCredential and in an open mandate, cnf.jwk is the agent's public key. The matching private key signs the agent's requests and closes open mandates.
    Content-Digest
    The HTTP header defined by RFC 9530 that carries a digest of the request body. The agent's signature covers it, which binds the signature to the exact body bytes.
    Idempotency-Key
    A request header, 22–64 printable ASCII characters, REQUIRED on every POST and PUT. A retry with the same key and body returns the stored response. See Identity and Request Signing.
    JCS
    JSON Canonicalization Scheme (RFC 8785). BACP hashes and signs JSON only in its JCS form.
    KB-JWT
    Key-binding JWT. A JWT signed with the agent's cnf key that proves the agent holds the key bound in a credential or open mandate. A KB-JWT for a seller carries aud = the seller's bpp_id and nonce = the Beckn confirm message id.
    Nonce
    A unique value in each request signature. The gateway records each (keyid, nonce) pair until the signature expires and rejects any repeat with replay_detected.
    SD-JWT
    Selective Disclosure JWT. A JWT format in which the holder discloses only the claims a verifier needs. AgentCredentials are SD-JWT VCs.
    Signature tag
    The tag parameter of an RFC 9421 request signature. It states what kind of action the request is. Each endpoint accepts exactly one agent tag, or one of two for negotiation messages, and the gateway rejects any other. The agent tags are bacp-browse, bacp-pay, bacp-post-order and bacp-negotiate. bacp-ts is used only by the Mandate Manager and PSP. See Identity and Request Signing.
    Status list
    A published list, indexed by credential or mandate, that shows whether each one is still valid. Verifiers check AgentCredentials and mandates against status lists.

    Mandates and consent¶

    Action scope
    The list of actions a mandate permits (action_scope): DISCOVER, QUOTE, ORDER, PAY, CANCEL, RETURN, RAISE_ISSUE and SHARE_DATA.
    Auth binding
    The auth_binding of a closed Payment Mandate. It states how this one payment is authorised: under the parent open mandate's rail binding (parent_rail_binding, for human-not-present payments), or by a fresh factor such as upi.pin (for human-present payments).
    Budget
    A periodic spending cap in a mandate's constraints.budgets[], with a period of P1D, P7D, P1M, FIRST_24H or LIFETIME. Budgets are decremented only from PSP-signed receipts, never from agent claims.
    Checkout Mandate
    A mandate that authorises what may be bought: categories, merchants, amounts, budgets, line items. The closed Checkout Mandate goes to the seller side. Compare Payment Mandate.
    Closed mandate
    A mandate for one specific checkout: exact amount, exact checkout hash, and a parent_mandate_id. In a human-not-present flow the agent closes the open mandate with its cnf key. In a human-present flow the principal signs it on the Trusted Surface.
    ConsentReceipt
    The DPDP record of what personal data goes to which recipient, for what purpose, in which language, with the withdrawal and grievance routes. One receipt per recipient per mandate. Schema: consent-receipt.json.
    Data scopes
    The personal-data fields a mandate allows the agent to share (data_scopes): NAME, PHONE, DELIVERY_ADDRESS, EMAIL, AGE_OVER_18 and GSTIN.
    Deterministic verifier
    The non-LLM code in the gateway that evaluates mandates, constraints, presence, rail caps and step-up rules authoritatively on every complete and as an advisory pre-check on every PUT /sessions/{id}, and fails closed. See Mandates and Consent.
    Mandate
    A signed, immutable statement of a principal's authority, structured like an AP2 mandate and behaving like an Account Aggregator consent artefact: purpose-coded, use-counted, revocable. Only status, usage and signatures change after signing. Schema: mandate.json.
    Mandate status
    One of PENDING, ACTIVE, PAUSED, REVOKED, EXPIRED, EXHAUSTED or REJECTED. Only an ACTIVE mandate can authorise a new commitment.
    Negotiation mandate
    A separate mandate (vct: in.bacp.mandate.negotiation.1, kind: negotiation) that holds a principal's negotiation bounds, including the reservation price. It is separate so that AP2-compatible verifiers, which fail closed on unknown constraints, keep working. Schema: mandate-negotiation.json.
    Open mandate
    A standing mandate with constraints rather than a fixed amount, signed by the principal on the Trusted Surface and countersigned by the Mandate Manager. It carries the agent's cnf key so that the agent can later close it. An open Payment Mandate MUST carry a rail binding.
    Payment Mandate
    A mandate that authorises how money may move. The closed Payment Mandate goes to the PSP, and carries no basket. Compare Checkout Mandate.
    Presence
    Whether the principal is present for an action: HP (human present) or HNP (human not present). It is declared on every session and every post-order write and carried in context.bacp, and each mandate lists the values it allows in presence_allowed.
    Purpose code
    A code such as P-GRO-01 (household grocery replenishment) that states what a mandate is for. Each purpose has bounds on amounts, budgets, categories, validity and data scopes, set in the purpose-code registry that the BACP working group governs (Q-47).
    Rail binding
    The rail_binding of an open Payment Mandate. It names the live standing authority the mandate draws on (for example a UPI Reserve Pay block or a UPI Circle delegation), its reference, the PSP, and a PSP-signed attestation of its limits, expiry and payee.
    Reservation ledger
    The gateway's per-mandate record of settled spends (from signed receipts) and in-flight reservations. Budget, usage and rail-cap checks count reservations as spent, inside one atomic section keyed on the root open Checkout Mandate.
    Revocation
    Ending a mandate, by the principal on the Trusted Surface, by the rail (for example a UPI safety switch), or by the agent relinquishing it. It reaches the gateway and the agent within 60 seconds. It stops new commitments; protective actions on orders already placed (cancel, return, grievance) remain allowed with presence: HP, as they do after a pause, expiry or exhaustion.
    Step-up
    Escalating a decision to the principal on the Trusted Surface. A step-up of kind confirm asks the principal to approve (for example, an amount above the mandate's threshold). A step-up of kind rail_auth needs a fresh payment factor (for example, a debit above an AFA threshold). The session goes to requires_escalation with a step_up object, and the link lives for at most 15 minutes.

    Payments¶

    AutoPay
    UPI AutoPay: a recurring UPI mandate identified by a UMN (Unique Mandate Number). Debits need a pre-debit notice at least 24 hours ahead, and debits above ₹15,000 (₹1,00,000 for insurance premiums, mutual funds and credit-card bills) need AFA. Handler in.upi.autopay.
    Binding class
    The class of a payment handler, which decides how it may be used: O (open-eligible), C (closed, human present only), A (assisted or device-bound), D (deferred, such as cash on delivery) or N (non-paying: financing, verification or tracking). See Handler Registry.
    Class-O handler
    A payment handler whose standing authority is enforced by the rail, the issuer or both, so that it MAY back an open (human-not-present) payment mandate. Examples: in.upi.reservepay, in.upi.circle, in.upi.autopay, in.upi.otm, in.card.mandate. A reserved handler such as in.upi.uap is class O but not yet eligible.
    COD
    Cash on delivery (in.cod), or UPI on delivery (in.cod.upi). A class-D handler: the order may be placed without the principal, but payment happens later with a person.
    Handler
    An identified way to pay, described as data in the handler registry. Canonical ids look like in.upi.reservepay. Older ids such as upi.reservepay.block are accepted as aliases during a deprecation window.
    Handler intersection
    The deterministic computation of which handlers can pay for a given deal: the registry, the gateway's profile, the seller's accepted handlers, the term sheet, the mandates, presence, amount and instrument availability, ordered by the principal's own preference.
    Notice-gated checkout
    The pattern for rails that need a pre-debit notice: the gateway issues the notice when the session is ready, refuses to move money until the notice matures, and cancels the session if the principal opts out.
    OTM
    UPI One-Time Mandate: a single block on the payer's account for one later capture by one merchant. Handler in.upi.otm.
    Pre-debit notice
    A notice to the payer before a mandate debit. It is required at least 24 hours ahead for e-mandate debits such as UPI AutoPay. Whether it applies to repeat Reserve Pay and OTM debits is open; BACP treats it as required from the second debit (Q-03).
    Receipt
    A PSP-signed record of a payment or refund, anchored to the rail's own reference (RRN, UTR or other rail evidence). Refund receipts also name the original payment. Schema: receipt.json.
    Reserve Pay / SBMD
    UPI Reserve Pay, the NPCI product built on RBI's single-block-multiple-debit (SBMD) facility: the payer blocks an amount once, with a UPI PIN, for one merchant, and that merchant may then debit the block several times until it is used or expires. In BACP a Reserve Pay block serves exactly one seller: its payee is that seller's collection VPA. Handler in.upi.reservepay.
    RRN
    Retrieval Reference Number. The 12-digit reference that identifies a UPI transaction across the payer PSP, NPCI and the banks. Disputes and refunds are joined on it.
    UDIR
    Unified Dispute and Issue Resolution, UPI's online dispute channel.
    UPI Circle
    UPI's delegated-payments feature. A primary user links a secondary (a person, or a device or software such as an AI agent) and sets limits. A primary user can add up to five secondary users. As of 17-10-2026, for devices and software NPCI publishes caps of ₹5,000 per transaction, ₹15,000 per month and ₹2,000 in the first 24 hours, per secondary user, and describes AI and software payments on Circle as a pilot, so the handler in.upi.circle has status pilot.
    UTR
    Unique Transaction Reference. The bank-side reference of a fund transfer, used with or instead of an RRN as settlement evidence.
    VPA
    Virtual Payment Address, a UPI id such as name@bank.

    Checkout, orders and evidence¶

    Checkout hash
    The hash of the seller-signed quote (from Beckn on_init). Closed mandates carry it, so the agent cannot pay for a different cart or amount than the seller signed. It is never the same thing as an offer hash.
    Checkout session
    The synchronous session that the gateway runs over asynchronous Beckn messages. Its statuses are incomplete, requires_escalation, ready_for_complete, complete_in_progress, completed, cancelled and human_handoff. See Sessions and Checkout.
    context.bacp
    The extension the gateway adds to the Beckn/ONDC context of every BACP-originated network message: agent, presence, mandate reference, idempotency key, channel, language, AI disclosure, instruction-log head and protocol version. Schema: beckn-context-bacp.json.
    Evidence bundle
    The signed package that lets a dispute be decided from evidence: the mandates, the seller-signed quote, receipts and the instruction-log chain. See Evidence and Instruction Log.
    IGM
    Issue and Grievance Management, ONDC's protocol for complaints. BACP targets IGM 2.0, which uses only issue and on_issue (issue_status and on_issue_status are IGM 1.x). BACP agents raise issues on the principal's behalf as an actors[] entry with type = AI_AGENT (Proposed: Q-11).
    In doubt
    The payment state when the PSP did not answer a debit request in time. The gateway reconciles with the PSP and attempts no new debit until the outcome is known.
    Instruction log
    The operator's signed, append-only, hash-chained record of prompt → intent → cart → mandate → payment reference for a session. Only hashes and references leave the operator. The gateway puts the current log head into context.bacp on every committing Beckn message. Schema: instruction-log-entry.json.
    ODR
    Online Dispute Resolution. An escalation path beyond IGM, referenced from an issue but outside the protocol.
    Post-order write
    A mandate-scoped action on an existing order: cancel, return or raise an issue. Each uses the signature tag bacp-post-order and needs the matching action in the mandate's action_scope.
    Reversal pending
    The payment state when money was debited but the seller never confirmed the order. The gateway starts a refund to source at once and tracks the rail's deadline.
    RSF
    Reconciliation and Settlement Framework, ONDC's protocol for reconciliation and settlement between network participants. When the buyer app collects, the gateway takes part as the collector. IGM outcomes feed RSF Correction objects. See Beckn / ONDC Binding.
    Webhook
    A gateway-signed notification to the agent's webhook_url, such as mandate.status_changed, session.updated, payment.receipt, order.updated, return.updated, issue.updated, pre_debit.notice or step_up.updated. Webhooks never embed signed receipts, only references with hashes.

    Negotiation¶

    Diff card
    The comparison shown only on the Trusted Surface when a negotiation needs the principal: the issue, the offered value, the principal's own bound, the all-in total and the offer expiry.
    Firm offer
    An offer with firm: true: a proposal that the counterparty can accept to form a contract. It is signed, has an absolute expiry, and may not be revoked after the counterparty dispatches its ACCEPT. Compare an indicative offer (firm: false), which is only an invitation to treat.
    Negotiation guard
    The deterministic, non-LLM check that runs on every outgoing firm counter-offer, ACCEPT, bid and award, and on every incoming offer. The LLM sees only its verdict: acceptable, not_acceptable or needs_escalation. See Negotiation.
    Negotiation mode
    How a negotiation runs: fixed, posted_dynamic, alternating, rfq_sealed, rfq_iterated, english, reverse_l1, dutch or scheme.
    Offer hash
    The SHA-256 hash of an offer in JCS form, excluding its signatures, countersignature and received_at. An ACCEPT carries the offer hash and nothing else about the terms.
    Reservation price
    The principal's walk-away price, held in the negotiation mandate with disclosure: "never". It never leaves the buyer side: no seller-facing message, webhook or log contains it. At or better than the reservation price the agent may accept on its own; between it and total_max the principal decides.
    Term sheet
    The structured, multi-issue terms of a deal: price, quantity, delivery, payment, returns, warranty, quality, schemes, deposits and more. Each negotiable issue carries a status of Assert, Agree, Disagree or Terminate. Schema: termsheet.json.
    within_bounds attestation
    A statement, signed by the buyer's credential provider (the Mandate Manager), that an ACCEPT is inside the buyer's negotiation mandate. The seller verifies only this attestation and never sees the bounds.

    Protocol and conformance¶

    Capability
    A named feature a participant supports, such as in.bacp.checkout, listed in its profile.
    Conformance level
    A named, testable set of requirements, such as bacp.core.1 (Core) or bacp.pay.upi.1 (Payments-UPI). See Conformance.
    Profile
    The JSON document a gateway publishes at /.well-known/bacp, listing its versions, transports, capabilities, payment handlers, keys and message_id_namespace. A conformance field is proposed. See Discovery and Profiles.
    Trade profile
    A named bundle of defaults for one Indian trade model (roles, negotiation modes, handlers, documents and validators). See Trade Profiles.
    Version
    A BACP release, named by its date in YYYY-MM-DD form. The first dated release is 2026-10-18. The unreleased working version on main is draft. See Versioning.
