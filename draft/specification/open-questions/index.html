    Open Questions¶

    Version 2026-10-18

    This page is part of BACP version 2026-10-18. Rules marked Proposed depend on an open question and may change in a later dated release.

    This page lists every question that BACP version 2026-10-18 cannot answer on its own, and the questions it has settled. Each open question names the body whose answer BACP needs and the working position this version takes until then. Other pages link here with anchors such as open-questions.md#q-16.

    No question blocks 2026-10-18

    No open question blocks version 2026-10-18. One, Q-77, is marked Blocking for settlement only: its fail-safe default applies, and the mark means that settling it needs the approvals that GOVERNANCE.md requires for a Blocking question. No other question is marked Blocking. A question is Blocking when, without its answer, a dated release cannot make a normative, testable statement, or would publish a design that conflicts with a rule already in force. Ten questions were Blocking before this release. Five are now settled with a concrete rule (Q-41, Q-42, Q-43, Q-44 and Q-47, plus Q-28 and Q-53, which were not Blocking). The other five (Q-12, Q-15, Q-16, Q-20 and Q-52) stay open but are non-blocking: each has a fail-safe default that the normative text states and that applies until the question is settled. A fail-safe default refuses, escalates to the principal on the Trusted Surface, or keeps a feature disabled; it never assumes the favourable answer.

    How open questions are handled. Every open question has a working position. Where a wrong guess could move money, release personal data or bind a party, the working position is a fail-safe default and is stated as a rule on the page that links here. An answer from the owner is applied in a later dated release under the versioning policy: a change that relaxes a fail-safe default is backwards-compatible; one that tightens it is a breaking change and gets the notice period. A newly found question that would block a normative, testable statement is marked Blocking here and must be resolved, or given a fail-safe default, before the next dated release.

    Owners. NPCI, RBI, ONDC, Legal counsel, MeitY/DPDP, or the BACP Working Group (BACP WG). Where several bodies are involved, the owner is the one that must give the answer.

    Questions¶

    Q-01 Liability for agent error¶

    Question. Who bears the loss when an agent mis-buys within a valid mandate: the agent operator, the buyer app, the PSP or the user?
    Owner. RBI, with NPCI and ONDC.

    Working position. BACP makes evidence complete by default (InstructionLog, receipts, evidence bundle) and makes no claim about the legal allocation. Proposed network policy: the operator remediates out-of-mandate actions without the user having to prove fault. See Evidence and Instruction Log.

    Q-02 Agent identity anchor¶

    Question. Which body anchors agent identity and issues the AgentCredential: an NPCI UAP registry, the ONDC registry or DeDi, or DIDs and VCs?
    Owner. ONDC, with NPCI.

    Working position. An issuer-agnostic SD-JWT VC, with the ONDC registry or DeDi as the default issuer, and a uap_agent_id slot reserved for NPCI. See Identity and Request Signing.

    Q-03 Pre-debit notice for Reserve Pay and OTM¶

    Question. Does the RBI E-mandate Framework 2026 pre-debit notice of at least 24 hours apply to Reserve Pay (single-block multi-debit) repeat debits, and to OTM? NPCI's Reserve Pay product page says the payer is "notified each time money is deducted" and mentions no pre-debit notice.
    Owner. NPCI.

    Working position. Fail-safe: an unknown pre-debit-notice status is treated as "required 24 h" from the second unattended debit on the block. See Rail Bindings. Findings of 18-10-2026: the UPI Consolidated Circular v1.0 section 15 (OTM) has no pre-debit notice (it requires post-debit notices, para 15.7), section 16 (Reserve Pay) has no authentication-factor clause, and the scope of the E-mandate Framework 2026 para 2 (recurring payments) for OTM is not confirmed.

    Q-04 Reserve Pay issuers and account types¶

    Question. Which issuers support Reserve Pay, and do any issuers apply a practice stricter than NPCI's rule of one block at a time per merchant per mobile number?
    Owner. NPCI.

    Working position. The account types are answered by NPCI UPI OC 228 (08-10-2025) and the UPI Consolidated Circular v1.0, para 16.1: all UPI-permitted sources of funds, including savings, current and overdraft accounts, RuPay credit cards and pre-sanctioned credit lines. OC 228 enables Reserve Pay, to begin with, only for "online verified merchants with low ticket and high frequency transactions". No public NPCI list of the issuers that support Reserve Pay was found as of 17-10-2026 (an absence finding, not a quotation), so the issuer list stays open, and a vendor page that reports issuers allowing only one block per user is not supported by any NPCI text found. BACP supports every account type, reads the source of funds (deposit or credit) from the PSP's attestation, and treats a credit-funded block as a credit leg. See Rail Bindings.

    Q-05 UPI 123PAY limit¶

    Question. RBI raised the UPI 123PAY per-transaction limit from ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 on 09-10-2024 and said NPCI would issue instructions. Has NPCI implemented ₹10,000 for every 123PAY channel?
    Owner. NPCI.

    Working position. Default ₹10,000, configurable in the handler descriptor.

    Q-06 IGM version¶

    Question. IGM 2.0 comments out the DISPUTE level, while escalation to ODR is current practice. Which version does BACP target?
    Owner. ONDC.

    Working position. Target IGM 2.0 and treat ODR as an out-of-protocol reference. See Beckn / ONDC Binding.

    Q-07 Member-only NPCI circulars¶

    Question. What do NPCI circulars OC 237, OC 238 and OC 141E, and the UPI Procedural Guidelines, say? They are available only to members.
    Owner. NPCI.

    Working position. Obtain them through a member bank. The upi.uap.* handler slot stays reserved.

    Q-08 UPI Circle details¶

    Question. What are the cooling period and the per-transaction cap for human secondary users of UPI Circle? NPCI's full-delegation release says only "a short cooling period".
    Owner. NPCI.

    Working position. Both are configurable in the handler descriptor.

    Q-09 context.bacp on ONDC¶

    Question. Will ONDC accept the context.bacp extension, or must it always be mirrored into message.order.tags[]?
    Owner. ONDC.

    Working position. Mirror it into a bacp TagGroup. See Beckn / ONDC Binding.

    Q-10 PSP verification of closed mandates¶

    Question. Will payer PSPs verify closed Payment Mandates and sign rail_binding attestations?
    Owner. NPCI, with PSPs.

    Working position. A PSP adapter inside the gateway's trust zone signs attestations. Start with a pilot with one PSP. Whether a payer PSP can report every debit on a Reserve Pay block to the gateway (a block-debit feed), and in what form, is part of this question; until it is answered, the pause reason unmatched_rail_debit applies only where a PSP attests such a feed (Usage ledger). The scope includes a modification of a block that the principal makes on the seller's platform (Consolidated Circular para 16.9): the optional rail.block_modified event is defined, and is used only where a feed is attested.

    Q-11 AI_AGENT in IGM¶

    Question. Will ONDC register AI_AGENT as an IGM 2.0 actor type (the type of an actors[] entry), given that the IGM 2.0 enum has only AGENT, which means a fulfilment agent?
    Owner. ONDC.

    Working position. A namespaced extension value, mirrored in tags.

    Q-12 Class consent receipts for unknown sellers¶

    Non-blocking: fail-safe default

    Was Blocking. The protocol rule is settled in 2026-10-18; the legal question stays open with the fail-safe default below. The default is relaxed only in a dated release (Fail-safe defaults); it is testable by conformance case T1-27 (cases T1-27 to T1-32 cover the six fail-safe defaults).

    Question. Does a class ConsentReceipt (recipient *) meet DPDP Rule 3 for human-not-present orders to sellers who are unknown when the mandate is set up? Must the per-seller receipt at init be normative before any data is released?
    Owner. MeitY/DPDP, with legal counsel.

    Settled in 2026-10-18. A per-seller ConsentReceipt, naming that seller's bpp_id, MUST be minted by the Mandate Manager at init before any personal data reaches the seller.     Q-19 Waiver of acceptance revocation¶

    Question. Can an offer waive the acceptor's right under Indian Contract Act s.5 to revoke an acceptance?
    Owner. Legal counsel.

    Findings. Section 5 reads: "An acceptance may be revoked at any time before the communication of the acceptance is complete as against the acceptor, but not afterwards." Under s.4 that communication is complete as against the acceptor "when it comes to the knowledge of the proposer". Neither section says "unless otherwise agreed", and no statute or other source found permits a waiver of that right. The Information Technology Act, 2000, s.10A covers "the revocation of proposals and acceptances" in electronic form; s.13 fixes, "save as otherwise agreed", only when an electronic record is despatched and received. The Uttar Pradesh amendment to s.5 (U.P. Act 57 of 1976) concerns proposals that must be kept open for a specified time, not acceptances.

    Working position (settled as design choice (c) by owner decision of 17-10-2026). A firm offer MUST state acceptance_revocation; there is no default. In this version every receiver refuses allowed_until_receipt with capabilities_incompatible, on every binding: a review of the matched-pair rule found that the proposer could still choose the receipt order after seeing both the acceptance and the revoke. The rules that would honour it are kept as sunset text, with the conditions under which a later version may lift the refusal. waived is permitted only with a signed waiver_basis (a contract or the buyer's own RFQ) and only outside consumer sessions; on Beckn, a waived offer with no basis, and in a consumer session any waived offer, may be accepted only by a human shown the consequence sentence. In a consumer session on REST, MCP or A2A no value is permitted, so only non-firm offers are possible there. The waiver may still not hold. See Acceptance revocation.

    Asked of counsel.

    1. Can a proposal, a protocol default or a prior framework agreement exclude the right under s.5, second paragraph?
    2. Is the time of receipt under the Information Technology Act, 2000, s.13(2) the "knowledge" of the proposer under s.4?
    3. Is a countersigned HTTP exchange an instantaneous communication in the sense of Bhagwandas Goverdhandas Kedia v. Girdharilal (1966 AIR 543)?
    4. Does the Uttar Pradesh Explanation to s.5 bind a quotation made in response to an RFQ?
    5. Does a waiver set by the offeror bind a human acceptor who joined only through a seller app's terms and conditions?
    6. Is a right that is advertised but not enforced worse, for the acceptor, than a waiver that is shown?
    7. Does a "delete for everyone" in a messaging app, or a call asking the other party to stop before the message is read, count as a revocation under ss.4–5?

    Protocol terms (18-10-2026). Every firm message now names protocol_terms{version, hash}, which state, among other things, the stipulation of IT Act s.12(2). Two further points for counsel: whether IT Act s.11 attribution makes a hash that an agent sends the principal's agreement for s.13, and whether a consumer's approval on the Trusted Surface that cites the terms is "otherwise agreed" under s.13.     (c) Acceptance revocation¶

    Settled

    Was proposed design choice (c), "acceptance revocation waived by default". Settled in 2026-10-18 by owner decision of 17-10-2026, amended the same day after review: allowed_until_receipt is refused everywhere, and the rules that would honour it are kept as sunset text.

    Question. Should every firm offer waive, by default, the acceptor's right under Indian Contract Act s.5 to revoke an acceptance before it reaches the proposer, or should the waiver be opt-in per offer?
    Owner. BACP WG; the validity of any waiver stays with Legal counsel (Q-19).

    Answer.

    • acceptance_revocation is REQUIRED on every firm offer, and there is no default. A gateway never inserts or normalises the value before hashing.
    • In this version every receiver refuses allowed_until_receipt with capabilities_incompatible, on every binding. The value stays in the schemas. A matched-pair rule that would honour it on the REST, MCP and A2A bindings is kept as sunset text: the revoke names the acceptance and the offer and is dispatched no earlier than the acceptance; the proposer appends a revoke it receives first to its transcript and holds it until 5 seconds after the revoke's dispatch; and the receiver returns its CounterSignature, with log_entry_hash, in the synchronous response to each message, so that it commits to receipt order before it sees the next message. A later version lifts the refusal only when the sunset conditions hold.
    • accept_revoke and withdraw are sent under bacp-negotiate. Every accept_revoke is countersigned, logged and refused (accept_revoke_not_allowed). Exposure release is idempotent, keyed on offer_hash.
    • waived needs a signed waiver_basis (a contract, or the buyer's own RFQ) that the acceptor's gateway obtained from its own principal before the offer arrived, that names the counterparty (np_id or GSTIN) and that the principal marked as a waiver basis, and is refused in consumer sessions: sessions whose profile is a consumer profile, that declare none, or whose buyer side acts for an individual or sends no acting_for. The profile is fixed when the session opens.
    • On Beckn legs, a waived offer with no basis, and in a consumer session any waived offer, may be accepted only by a human shown the consequence sentence; the only schema error excused is the missing basis. In a consumer session on REST, MCP or A2A no value is permitted, so only non-firm offers are possible there.
    • A negotiation approval is posted as negotiation.step_up.approved with Presentation.offer_hash, never with checkout_hash.
    • The Trusted Surface shows the value only as a fixed consequence sentence followed by the cancellation terms, never as "cannot cancel". BACP does not yet say which value an offer SHOULD use.
    • RFQ awards have no fixed value. The comparison set is frozen at close (for rfq_iterated, the close of the final shortlist round); a revoked award, possible only under the sunset text, cancels the RFQ or auction (NEG_COLLUSION_SCREEN), and it cannot be awarded again (NEG_AWARD_RULE). accept_revoke never governs bid_retract.

    See Acceptance revocation, Award and Trade Profiles.

    (d) Multi-seller hands-free shopping¶

    Settled

    Was proposed design choice (d). Settled in 2026-10-18 by owner decision of 17-10-2026.

    Question. Given that a Reserve Pay block pays one seller, in what order does an agent fall back when it buys from several sellers with no human present, and what does each fallback mean for the principal?
    Owner. BACP WG.

    Answer.

    • A multi-seller purchase is a basket of sessions, one per seller, grouped by a gateway-local basket_id. No payment spans sellers, and there is no atomicity across sellers.
    • Per leg, the gateway plans the payment in this order: the principal's designated preference; then the seller's existing payee-bound instrument, such as a block already held for that seller (3a); then a gift PPI the principal designated; then one planned human-present step, raised for the whole basket before any debit. No rung ranks above the principal's preference.
    • A new per-seller block (3b) is offered only beside a one-time human-present payment to the same seller, with equal weight, never after a failed payment and never on an IVR callback. A block set up outside a checkout is exempt.
    • Every sibling Payment Mandate sits under one root open Checkout Mandate; a block names its payee (payee{vpa, bpp_id}), bound to the seller's signed payment terms; the blocks under a root sum to no more than its smallest budget; each block counts as reserved spend at its full amount; revoking the root revokes each block.
    • A change of a leg's funding class (own deposit, prepaid or borrowed), fee-bearer, payee or collector raises a rail_fallback step-up; an in_doubt leg is never re-routed.

    Settled on 18-10-2026 (owner decision, panel round R2, topic T7).

    Question. Should split-order detection add a cross-seller velocity signal keyed on (mandate, window) instead of the seller?
    Owner. BACP WG.

    Answer. Split-order detection is keyed on the root open Checkout Mandate and the seller, within 10 minutes, and counts completed and in-flight orders. The seller is identified by the tax id in the BPP-signed terms, else by the payee VPA, else by the pair bpp_id and provider_id. The covers[] legs of a summary-approved basket are exempt, because the principal has approved each of them. A cross-seller velocity signal is not added: velocity across different sellers is bounded by the budgets. See Step-up and Atomic reservation.

    Q-46 Exposure of credit-ledger deals¶

    Settled

    Settled on 18-10-2026 (owner decision, panel round R2, topic T7).

    Question. Should the cross-session exposure check count B2B credit-ledger deals against the checkout mandate's budgets or against credit_ledger.max_outstanding?
    Owner. BACP WG.

    Answer. A credit-ledger sale counts against the checkout mandate's budgets and against credit_ledger.max_outstanding only when it is booked by a gateway complete under the buyer's mandate, inside the root's atomic section. A sale that the creditor posts never touches the budgets. A disputed entry keeps its count until it is resolved or credit-noted. A payment that settles a sale counts within the sale, never on top of it, and releases max_outstanding only; a credit_note releases both. See Booking and settling.

    Q-59 Merchant- and BACP-enforced handlers¶

    Settled

    Settled on 18-10-2026 (owner decision, panel round R2, topic T7), as closed: not open-eligible in this release.

    Question. May an open payment mandate bind to in.store_credit, in.loyalty or bacp.credit_ledger, whose limits are enforced by the merchant or by BACP rather than by a rail or an issuer? If so, who may sign their attestations and receipts when the payee is also the party that acts, and how does a credit-ledger sale, which has no payment receipt, resolve its budget reservation?
    Owner. BACP WG.

    Answer. in.store_credit, in.loyalty and bacp.credit_ledger are not open-eligible in this release, and their descriptors have presence.hnp: none, because the limits of each are enforced by the merchant or by BACP and not by a rail or an issuer. A principal who wants to pay human-not-present from store credit, loyalty or a khata ledger cannot yet; human-present payment is unaffected. The conditions for opening them are written down now, so that opening later is a registry change plus an owner decision:

    Proposed

    A handler whose limits a merchant or BACP enforces MAY back an open mandate only when all of these hold, each tested by a conformance case (T7-40 and the cases of the handler-binding cases):

    1. Attestations and receipts are never signed by a key of the payee's operator.
    2. A failure receipt signed by the payee keeps the reservation counted as spent until the principal confirms on the Trusted Surface.
    3. A credit-ledger acknowledgement comes from the principal's or the Mandate Manager's key, never the agent's cnf key.
    4. A store-credit debit carries the merchant-signed balance_before and balance_after.
    5. A loyalty redemption carries a programme-signed conversion_value.

    Whether a gateway that funds a ledger needs RBI registration under the RBI Act, 1934, s.45-IA is a question for counsel (not verified as of 18-10-2026). See Handler binding and Booking and settling.

    Q-82 Pinned UCP release for the UCP checkout projection¶

    Settled

    Settled on 18-10-2026 (owner decision, panel round R2, topic T4 with the T8 apply).

    Question. Should the in.bacp.ucp_checkout projection pin a tagged release of the Universal Commerce Protocol or a commit?
    Owner. BACP WG.

    Answer. The projection pins UCP's tagged release v2026-08-25 (25-08-2026, commit cd78fb3), the latest of UCP's four releases as of 18-10-2026. The pin was commit a5e7cfe (12-10-2026) on UCP's main branch, which upstream labels draft and which is 39 commits after the tag; four of the 41 pinned files differ, so the move changes the copies of link.json, policy.json, warning_code.json and ucp.json. Three changes that UCP made on main after the tag are not in the pinned release, and BACP does not depend on them: the Ask capability, the cart rule that line_items SHOULD be [], and the registry of policy types (the tagged text lists dev.ucp.shopping.policy.return and .warranty). A gateway MUST NOT claim UCP compatibility on any other UCP version. A later re-pin to a newer tag re-runs the projection checks. The projection's handler keys are the UCP aliases dev.bacp.<handler id>, each with a schema at https://bacp.dev/schemas/ucp/<handler id>.json (decided 18-10-2026, after the owner confirmed that BACP holds bacp.dev); the old keys stay accepted for the dated release that introduces the alias. See UCP checkout projection.

    Other settled points¶

    Question Answer
    Lifetime of single-use payment tokens and step-up links At most 15 minutes.
    May a buyer app hold one Reserve Pay block for many sellers? No: one block per seller ((d)).
    How does a 24-hour AutoPay notice fit a 10-minute quote? The notice-gated checkout pattern in Rail Bindings.
    Which header carries the credential, and what does the signature cover? BACP-Agent; see Identity and Request Signing.
    Is a rail-cap breach an escalation or an error? Only AFA thresholds escalate; other caps return 403 rail_limit_exceeded (Errors).
    How are colliding handler aliases resolved? Exact beats glob; see Handler Registry. Naming governance is settled in Q-28.
    What evidence do non-UPI receipts carry? rail.evidence keyed by the descriptor's evidence references; see Rail Bindings.
    Does BACP carry fee allocation (MDR, aggregator commission, finder fee, settlement_eta)? No (18-10-2026). BACP makes normative what the principal pays and to whom, and keeps the seller's own costs informative (Who pays).
    Is a seller or agent human_confirmation_required field adopted? No (18-10-2026). Sellers already refuse unattended payment through accepted_handlers and COD refusal. The guarded alternative is the seller requires_principal_confirmation profile, which MUST NOT change a price or a handler (Discovery).
    Is BACP wire-compatible with UCP checkout? No (18-10-2026). Its checkout lifecycle and status names follow UCP's (BACP spells cancelled and adds human_handoff), but paths, totals and the sign of discounts, the ucp envelope, the complete payload and payment authority, and the MCP argument shape differ. The earlier claim of wire compatibility is retracted, and the differences are listed in BACP and other protocols.
    Is a match code (ts_match_code) shown on the Trusted Surface? A MAY, guarded (18-10-2026). It is derived from the render's content_hash, shown on app or web only, never read on IVR or UPI 123PAY, never a voice challenge, never a control and never evidence, and it is a MAY because a phishing page simply shows no code (Human participants).
    Is a Trusted Surface page allowed to take a UPI PIN? No (18-10-2026), as BACP policy: a PIN is taken only in the PSP's own app or on a call that the principal places to the PSP's published number, never on a web page and never on a call that the Trusted Surface places. That NPCI rules already say so is not verified as of 18-10-2026 (PIN rule).
    Is per-mandate evaluation atomic? Yes, within one gateway, keyed on the root open checkout mandate (Security and Privacy); across gateways see Q-36.
